Fuel Watch structural correction.

Use the previous APAC Fuel Risk Report as the structural benchmark, but keep the newer Polestar Watch cover and branding.

Key correction:
Hard Numbers should sit where Fast Facts currently sits.

Do not have a weak generic Fast Facts block followed later by Hard Numbers. For Fuel Watch, the top metric section should be called:

HARD NUMBERS

It should appear immediately after the Executive Summary, in the position currently occupied by Fast Facts.

1. Replace Fast Facts with Hard Numbers

Remove the current generic Fast Facts block for Fuel Watch.

Do not show weak cards such as:
- Mixed fuel reporting
- Data quality issue
- generic Total Records only
- latest incident only
- most affected country only

Instead, Fuel Watch should show a Hard Numbers section near the top of the report.

Hard Numbers should include the strongest available fuel indicators, for example:
- Brent crude price
- WTI crude price
- Singapore jet fuel benchmark
- diesel / petrol price movement where available
- refinery outages
- fuel shortage count
- subsidy / levy changes
- producer or buyer actions
- chokepoint or route disruption count where fuel-relevant
- highest severity
- reporting period

Only show metrics where data exists or can be calculated credibly.
Do not invent live market data.
If a metric is not available, omit it rather than showing a dash.

2. Restore the Jet Fuel Price Trajectory chart

Fuel Watch must include the Jet Fuel Price Trajectory chart where data exists.

The previous report included:
- Jet Fuel Price Trajectory
- Singapore benchmark
- last 30 days
- price movement line
- date markers / annotations

Bring this concept back into the current Fuel Watch format.

Chart rules:
- Use the Polestar chart style.
- Roboto fonts.
- Approved brand colours only.
- Clean axis labels.
- No decorative clutter.
- No fake data.
- If there is no jet fuel time-series data available, show a clear empty-state note:
  “Jet fuel trajectory data is not available for this reporting cycle.”
- Do not replace the chart with generic incident counts.

3. Fuel Watch section order

Use this order:

- Cover
- Executive Summary
- Hard Numbers
- Jet Fuel Price Trajectory
- Situation
- What Happened
- Regional Highlights
- Producer and Buyer Actions
- What Matters
- Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View
- Related Incidents
- Source Notes / Data Notes
- Disclaimer

If a section has no data, omit it or show a short clear empty-state note. Do not fill it with generic prose.

4. Keep Fuel Watch analytical, not just incident-based

Fuel Watch should not be only a list of fuel incidents.

It should combine:
- incident reporting
- market indicators
- price movement
- fuel shortage signals
- refinery or supply disruption
- chokepoint pressure
- producer and buyer actions
- business impact

The previous report had more useful analytical depth. Restore that depth without copying its wording exactly.

5. Related Incidents filtering

Related Incidents should support the Fuel Watch analysis.

Prioritise:
- pump price movement
- fuel shortages
- rationing
- refinery disruption
- fuel levy / subsidy changes
- tanker or forecourt disruption
- generator fuel exposure
- transport fuel disruption
- direct business continuity impact

Demote or exclude:
- generic oil price commentary
- pure market speculation
- company finance stories
- articles with no operational fuel relevance

6. Branding and fonts

Roboto is mandatory in preview and PDF.

Use only:
- Midnight Blue: #0b0a3d
- Electric Blue: #465bff
- Dusk Gray: #363636
- Polar Gray: #e2e2e2

Do not use:
- #0B0B3D
- #4655FF
- #303030
- #E2E2E2
