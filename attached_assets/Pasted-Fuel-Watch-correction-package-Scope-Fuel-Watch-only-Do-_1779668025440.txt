2. Hard Numbers must be about fuel prices, supply and market pressure

Hard Numbers must not be a renamed Fast Facts block.

The current Hard Numbers section is still too incident-count based. For Fuel Watch, Hard Numbers must primarily show actual fuel price, supply and cost-pressure indicators.

Hard Numbers should answer:

“What are the actual fuel price and supply-pressure indicators this week?”

Prioritise:

Price indicators:
- Brent crude price
- WTI crude price
- jet fuel price
- diesel price movement
- petrol price movement
- pump price changes by country where available
- fuel surcharge movement where available

Supply indicators:
- refinery outages
- fuel shortages
- rationing events
- forecourt disruption
- tanker / fuel transport disruption
- generator fuel exposure
- stockpile / reserve movement where available

Policy and cost indicators:
- subsidy changes
- fuel levy changes
- excise / duty / tax changes
- government price controls
- import/export restrictions affecting fuel

Route and chokepoint indicators:
- Hormuz / Red Sea / Malacca fuel-relevant disruption
- fuel cargo delays
- tanker route disruption
- war-risk or insurance pressure affecting fuel movement

Incident counts may appear, but only as supporting metrics. They must not dominate Hard Numbers.

Do not use Hard Numbers for weak cards such as:
- Fuel Incidents
- Most Affected Country
- Latest Incident

Those belong elsewhere if needed.

If price data is not available from a live feed, support manual entry through hardNumbers jsonb.

Omit metrics with no data. Do not show dashes. Do not invent values.

Do not assume the benchmark must be Singapore.

Acceptable benchmark options include:
- IATA / Platts Jet Fuel Price Monitor if available and usable
- regional jet fuel benchmark
- global jet fuel benchmark
- Asia jet fuel benchmark
- Singapore jet fuel benchmark
- US Gulf Coast kerosene-type jet fuel
- EIA / FRED jet fuel series
- manually entered jet fuel series stored in hardNumbers jsonb

Source preference:
1. Existing internal project data if already available
2. hardNumbers jsonb manual series
3. public structured source such as EIA / FRED
4. IATA / Platts summary data only if usable without breaching licensing or scraping restrictions

Do not scrape paid Platts data.
Do not fake jet fuel prices.
Do not use old values unless they are deliberately stored as the report cycle’s data.

8. Regional Highlights must be analyst prose

Do not output raw headline dumps.

Current weak style:
“Iran: 9 records in window. StanChart Says Record SPR Withdrawals…”

That is not a regional highlight.

Use analyst prose:
“Iran remains the main pressure point, with reporting tied to Hormuz disruption, pricing pressure and supply-risk narratives.”

Rules:
- Do not show “Unknown” as a regional highlight.
- Records without clear location stay in totals and source notes only.
- Country highlights should explain the fuel signal, not paste article headlines.
- Use concise country-level paragraphs or a clean table.

9. Producer and Buyer Actions need proper classification

Do not label everything as “Producer”.

Use categories:
- Producer action
- Buyer action
- Government / policy action
- Infrastructure / routing action
- Market / supply signal

Examples:
- “Parliamentary caucus warns against using fuel levy…” = Government / policy action
- “ADNOC pipeline bypassing Hormuz…” = Infrastructure / routing action
- “Indian Oil increasing spot purchases…” = Buyer action
- “Saudi Aramco maintaining export volumes…” = Producer action

Render as a table if possible:
Actor / Category / Action / Date

If actor is unknown, use:
Government / policy signal
or omit actor.

Do not output a loose bullet list of weakly classified headlines.
