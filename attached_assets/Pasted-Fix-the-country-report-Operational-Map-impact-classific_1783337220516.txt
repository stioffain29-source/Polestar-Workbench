Fix the country report Operational Map impact classification.

The map structure is improving, but the impact levels are still wrong.

Current problem:
Several crime or security incidents are being labelled “Direct impact” even though they do not show a direct commercial business impact. A murder case, shooting, drug raid, arrest, local clash, or general criminal incident should not be marked Direct impact unless the reporting shows a clear operational effect on business activity.

Core rule:
Direct impact means the incident is affecting, or is very likely to affect, commercial operations now.

Use Direct impact only when reporting shows one or more of the following:
- road closure or access restriction
- protest blocking access
- port, airport, warehouse, factory or site disruption
- utility outage affecting operations
- fire or explosion affecting an industrial, commercial or logistics area
- labour action affecting production, transport, port access or site access
- regulatory enforcement affecting operations, permits, movement, inspections or site activity
- security incident directly affecting a business site, staff, contractor movement, delivery route or transport corridor
- confirmed disruption to logistics, production, warehousing, customer delivery or staff movement

Use Possible impact when:
- the incident is near an operating area, route, industrial zone or business district but no actual disruption is confirmed
- the incident could affect staff confidence, local movement, security posture or monitoring requirements
- the issue is relevant, but the operational effect is indirect or uncertain
- the reporting indicates security or crime activity but no business interruption is reported

Use Monitor only when:
- the incident is isolated
- the incident is mainly criminal, political or localised with no clear business consequence
- the event is outside key commercial areas or routes
- no movement, access, site, logistics, utility or continuity impact is reported

Do not write “regulatory and compliance exposure” unless the incident is actually regulatory or compliance related.
Do not write “operational monitoring relevance” unless there is a clear reason to monitor it.

This map shows reported operationally relevant issues for the current reporting period. Not every security or crime incident creates commercial impact. Direct impact is used only where reporting indicates a current effect on movement, access, sites, logistics, utilities, production or business continuity. Acceptance criteria:
- No murder, arrest, drug raid or isolated crime incident is labelled Direct impact unless a commercial operational effect is stated.
- Direct impact is reserved for actual business disruption or clear immediate operational effect.
- Possible impact is used for relevant but indirect issues.
- Monitor only is used for isolated security or crime reporting with no business disruption.
- Business relevance wording must match the incident.
