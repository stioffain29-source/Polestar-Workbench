REFINE THE SELECTED INCIDENTS SECTION

The Selected Incidents section requires further revision.

Do not remove the section, but make it more informative and credible.


1. REMOVE CONFIDENCE FROM THE CLIENT DISPLAY

Remove the visible:

“Confidence: Low”
“Confidence: Medium”
“Confidence: High”

from each incident card.

Confidence should remain stored in the underlying record and remain available within the Workbench, but it should not appear prominently in the standard client PDF.

The current placement beside the severity badge makes the entry appear contradictory and undermines confidence in the overall report.

Retain the severity badge, but label it clearly as:

SEVERITY: HIGH
SEVERITY: MODERATE
SEVERITY: LOW

Do not show an unlabelled coloured badge.


2. RENAME THE SECTION

Change:

SELECTED INCIDENTS

to:

KEY INCIDENTS

Add the subtitle:

“Incidents that best illustrate the main operational patterns identified during the reporting period.”


3. SHOW FEWER, BETTER INCIDENTS

Show a maximum of four key incidents.

Do not automatically select six incidents.

Each selected incident must add something distinct to the report.

The four incidents should normally represent:

1. The dominant incident pattern
2. The most operationally significant incident
3. A different affected geography or supply chain stage
4. A meaningful arrest, recovery or disruption outcome

Do not select several nearly identical incidents from the same country and category unless they demonstrate a genuine cluster.


4. IMPROVE EACH INCIDENT CARD

Each card should contain:

DATE AND LOCATION

Example:

11 July 2026 | Gazaria, Bangladesh

INCIDENT TYPE AND SEVERITY

Example:

Cargo theft in transit | Severity: High

WHAT HAPPENED

Use two concise sentences explaining:

1. What was targeted
2. How the incident occurred
3. What cargo or property was lost
4. Whether violence, weapons or organised activity were reported
5. Any confirmed value or quantity
6. The immediate outcome

OPERATIONAL RELEVANCE

Add one short line explaining why the incident matters to cargo owners or logistics operators.

Example:

“Demonstrates exposure on familiar highway routes and the vulnerability of vehicles moving without additional journey controls.”

STATUS

Where known, show one of:

Ongoing
Suspects arrested
Cargo recovered
Under investigation
Unconfirmed

Do not show a status where none is available.


5. EXAMPLE CARD FORMAT

11 JULY 2026 | GAZARIA, BANGLADESH

CARGO THEFT IN TRANSIT                         SEVERITY: HIGH

A commercial vehicle was intercepted beside the highway and goods reportedly valued at Tk 2 million were taken. The reporting indicates a planned roadside robbery, although the involvement of weapons and the recovery status were not confirmed.

Operational relevance:
Highlights exposure during road movement on predictable routes and the need for journey monitoring and escalation procedures.

Status: Under investigation


6. VALIDATE THAT INCIDENTS ARE ACTUALLY CARGO RELATED

Before including an incident, confirm that it involves at least one of the following:

1. Commercial cargo
2. A freight vehicle
3. A warehouse, depot or distribution centre
4. A port, terminal or cargo yard
5. A commercial vessel
6. A logistics operator
7. A commercial consignment
8. Cargo handling or custody

Exclude ordinary robberies involving:

1. Private travellers
2. Personal mobile phones
3. Personal cash
4. Passenger vehicles
5. Household property

unless the event clearly involved a commercial cargo movement or logistics operation.

Do not classify every highway robbery as Cargo theft in transit.


7. DEDUPLICATE SIMILAR INCIDENTS

The two entries dated 08 July describing travellers being robbed appear to describe the same or closely related reporting.

Ensure that different reports of the same event are merged into one incident.

Deduplication should consider:

1. Date
2. Location
3. Method
4. Vehicle or cargo involved
5. Loss description
6. Named suspects or victims
7. Similarity of the incident narrative

Do not select two versions of the same incident.


8. INCIDENT SELECTION MUST BE ANALYTICAL

Do not simply select the latest four records.

Rank eligible incidents using:

1. Operational significance
2. Relevance to the report’s main patterns
3. Severity
4. Confirmed commercial loss
5. Violence or weapons
6. Business interruption
7. Organised or insider indicators
8. Law enforcement outcome
9. Geographic and category diversity

The selected incidents should support the report’s assessment rather than fill space.


9. DISPLAY REQUIREMENTS

1. Keep the current clean card layout.
2. Allow slightly more vertical space for each incident.
3. Use readable body text.
4. Do not shrink the wording to fit all cards on one page.
5. Do not print full media headlines.
6. Do not print confidence scores.
7. Do not print source counts.
8. Do not print repeated missing-data statements.
9. Keep each card to approximately 80 words maximum.
10. Prevent cards from splitting across pages.


10. FINAL CHECK

After making these changes:

1. Confirm all displayed incidents are genuine cargo or logistics incidents.
2. Confirm no duplicate incidents are shown.
3. Confirm every card explains what happened and why it matters.
4. Confirm confidence remains available internally but is absent from the standard PDF.
5. Confirm severity is clearly labelled.
6. Confirm the Key Incidents section contains no more than four entries.
7. Export and inspect the PDF at normal page size.
8. Do not change Supply Chain Exposure or Operational Patterns.
