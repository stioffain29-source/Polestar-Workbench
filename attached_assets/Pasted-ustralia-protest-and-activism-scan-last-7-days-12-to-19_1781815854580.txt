ustralia protest and activism scan: last 7 days, 12 to 19 June 2026

Overall picture: activity is localised but politically charged. Main themes are Palestine related protest enforcement, anti One Nation mobilisation, public housing activism, anti defence industry organising, and industrial action in energy and public services. No sign of nationwide civil unrest, but several events had heavy police presence or legal follow up.

Brisbane, 14 June: Seven people were arrested at a pro Palestine rally in King George Square after allegedly using or displaying banned phrases under Queensland’s hate speech laws. Around 300 attended. Queensland Police described the overall behaviour of protesters as peaceful, but the arrests have drawn criticism from Amnesty and protest groups.

Melbourne, 12 June: Pauline Hanson’s One Nation fundraiser drew anti racism protesters after a venue change. Demonstrators gathered outside the South Melbourne venue with banners, drums and chants. Police were deployed; Victoria Police later said no arrests were made, though one man was directed to move on. Hanson and Barnaby Joyce reportedly left by a rear exit.

Canberra, 17 June: Hanson’s National Press Club address was disrupted by a GetUp banner stunt. The National Press Club referred the incident to the AFP. This is more activist disruption than street protest, but it has become a live political flashpoint around One Nation, multiculturalism, wages and campaign tactics.

Sydney, Waterloo: Public housing activists continued opposition to demolition and redevelopment at Waterloo South. Reporting points to a small but persistent protest camp and standoff, with earlier numbers around 30 to 40 demonstrators blocking contractors and police involvement around fencing and demolition preparations.

Industrial action: The major operational impact was in energy. Unions ended strike action at INPEX Ichthys LNG after a pay and conditions deal; Reuters reported the strike had disrupted LNG exports and shut one of two production trains. Separately, BHP Port Hedland workers were threatening strike action over pay, while Melbourne council workers had planned strike activity around 17 June.

Perth watch item: WA is preparing expanded police powers and a protected zone around the upcoming Land Forces defence expo in Perth, clearly influenced by previous defence expo protests in Melbourne. This is not yet a protest event in the last seven days, but it is a strong indicator of expected anti war and anti defence industry mobilisation.

Assessment: Moderate activism level, low wider unrest risk. The highest likelihood of disruption is around politically sensitive events involving One Nation, Palestine related rallies in Queensland, housing demolition sites in Sydney, and defence industry events in WA. The strongest business impact in the period came from industrial action rather than street protest.
