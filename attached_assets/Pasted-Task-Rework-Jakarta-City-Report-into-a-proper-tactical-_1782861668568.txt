Task: Rework Jakarta City Report into a proper tactical operating product

The latest Jakarta report is still too thin and generic. It has the right section headings, but the content is not yet tactical enough for a paying client. It currently reads like a lightly enhanced city summary, not an operational brief.

Do not rebuild the whole app. Fix the Jakarta report generation logic, content depth and map output.

Main problem

The report must stop producing generic phrases such as:

“Allow extra time”
“Check routes”
“Maintain caution”
“Movement may be disrupted”
“Confirm locally before travel”
“Build time buffers”

These phrases are only useful if they are tied to:

named locations
named roads
known business districts
airport corridors
port access routes
warehouse and logistics exposure
actual incident locations
specific operating actions
clear escalation triggers

Required output standard

The Jakarta report must answer these questions clearly:

Can staff move today?
Which areas should they avoid or approach with caution?
Which roads or corridors are likely to be affected?
Are airport transfers exposed?
Are office, hotel, mall or meeting areas exposed?
Are port, cargo, trucking or warehouse movements affected?
What should drivers, security teams and logistics teams do before dispatch?
What would trigger delay, reroute, cancellation or escalation?
Rewrite the report logic

Each section must be driven by incident data and named locations. If the data does not support a claim, do not pad it with generic text.

Use this rule:

No named location, no operational claim.

For example:

Bad:
“Protest activity may disrupt movement in Central Jakarta.”

Good:
“Protest activity near the Central Jakarta government district may affect access around Gambir, Monas, Istana Merdeka, Jl. Medan Merdeka and connecting roads toward Thamrin. Meetings nearby should be checked before departure and avoided during active police cordons.”

Bad:
“Port congestion may affect delivery windows.”

Good:
“Any disruption around Tanjung Priok, Cilincing, Koja or North Jakarta access roads can affect container collection, truck staging and onward movement toward Bekasi, Cikarang, Tangerang and Greater Jakarta warehouse areas.”

Tactical Operating Picture

This must be short but specific.

It should say:

where the risk sits
what type of disruption it creates
who it affects
what the immediate operating consequence is

Example:

“Jakarta remains operational, but movement planning should focus on Central Jakarta protest exposure, North Jakarta port access, and weather related disruption on cross city and airport routes. The main business impact is not city wide deterioration, but short notice disruption to meetings, port collections, warehouse dispatches and staff transfers.”

Escalation Triggers

Make this a decision tool.

Use clear triggers:

Active protest near Monas, Istana Merdeka, DPR/MPR or Gambir
Police cordons or road closures on Thamrin, Sudirman, Medan Merdeka or Gatot Subroto
Flooding affecting low clearance vehicles
Airport transfer time exceeding normal planning window
Tanjung Priok terminal or gate disruption
Truck queues or staging outside secure areas
Incidents near client offices, hotels, warehouses or malls
Driver cannot confirm two viable routes
Security provider advises delay or reroute

Recommended Actions

Actions must be role based.

Use this format:

For travellers

Confirm route before departure. Avoid non essential movement near active protest sites. Keep meetings flexible if travelling through Central Jakarta.

For security teams

Check protest, flood and police activity before morning and afternoon movement windows. Escalate if disruption affects office, hotel, airport or port routes.

For logistics teams

Confirm Tanjung Priok terminal status, truck availability and road conditions before dispatch. Avoid roadside staging outside secure areas.

For local management

Review critical meetings, deliveries and visitor movements each morning. Decide whether to proceed, delay or reroute based on confirmed local conditions.
