Create two new weekly intelligence products:

POLESTAR APAC WEEKLY
Security, Risk & Operational Intelligence

POLESTAR MIDDLE EAST WEEKLY
Security, Risk & Operational Intelligence

PURPOSE

These are regional intelligence products, not general news summaries.

The reports must identify what materially changed during the reporting period, explain why it matters operationally, and identify what should be watched during the following seven days.

Do not fill the report with every event collected by the system. Only include developments that are materially relevant to:

* Personnel safety and security
* Business travel
* Site and asset security
* Supply-chain continuity
* Ports, airports and transport
* Energy and fuel
* Business continuity
* Regulatory or political disruption
* Armed conflict
* Civil unrest
* Significant crime or organised crime
* Corporate operational exposure

The final published report should be curated. Most scraped incidents should not appear unless they materially change the regional risk picture.

REPORT STRUCTURE

1. BLUF — REGIONAL OUTLOOK

150–200 words.

Summarise:

* What materially changed this week
* Whether the regional operating environment is improving, stable or deteriorating
* The 3–4 most important issues affecting businesses, travellers or operations
* Any major change from the previous reporting period

Do not simply repeat headlines.

2. KEY DEVELOPMENTS

Maximum 6–8 developments.

Each development must use:

COUNTRY | SHORT EVENT TITLE

Severity: Insignificant / Low / Moderate / High / Extreme

What happened:
100–150 words describing the confirmed development in concise prose.

Why it matters:
50–80 words explaining the implications for personnel, operations, travel, logistics, assets, business continuity or corporate exposure.

Outlook:
A short assessment of what should be watched over the next seven days.

Only include significant developments. Avoid minor incidents unless they indicate a wider trend.

3. REGIONAL SECURITY PICTURE

Provide a concise assessment of meaningful changes involving:

* Armed conflict
* Terrorism
* Civil unrest and activism
* Political instability
* Significant crime or organised crime
* Interstate tensions

Do not provide a generic country-by-country threat summary.

Focus on what changed during this reporting period.

4. BUSINESS & OPERATIONAL RISK

Identify developments affecting:

* Airports and airspace
* Ports and maritime operations
* Road transport
* Border crossings
* Fuel and energy
* Electricity and utilities
* Telecommunications
* Supply chains
* Cargo security
* Regulatory developments
* Business continuity

Only include issues with credible operational implications.

5. TRAVEL & PERSONNEL

Summarise new or changed considerations affecting:

* Business travel
* Personnel movement
* Airport access
* Ground transport
* Demonstrations
* Security restrictions
* Curfews
* Border controls
* Significant localised security deterioration

Do not repeat standing travel advice unless circumstances have changed.

6. 7-DAY WATCHLIST

Maximum 5–8 entries.

Use the following fields:

Location
Issue
Current Severity
What We Are Watching

The watchlist should identify credible developments or triggers that could materially affect operations during the following seven days.

Examples include:

* Scheduled protests
* Elections
* Court rulings
* Military anniversaries
* Political deadlines
* Planned strikes
* Major religious or public events
* Conflict escalation indicators
* Infrastructure disruption
* Weather events with operational consequences

7. POLESTAR OUTLOOK

Approximately 150–200 words.

Provide forward-looking analysis covering:

* What is most likely to matter during the next seven days
* Where conditions may deteriorate
* Which indicators could change the current assessment
* Potential operational consequences

This section must add analysis rather than repeat earlier sections.

EDITORIAL RULES

* BLUF first
* Plain English
* Incident details written in prose
* No excessive bullet dumping
* No generic news aggregation
* No filler
* No duplication between sections
* Prioritise operational relevance
* Separate confirmed facts from assessment
* Do not exaggerate severity
* Severity reflects the current incident or situation, not the highest theoretical risk
* Use only: Insignificant / Low / Moderate / High / Extreme
* Prefer developments from the reporting period
* Older events should only appear when there has been a material new development
* Distinguish isolated incidents from wider trends
* Focus on what changed
* Explain why developments matter to businesses
* Avoid speculative conclusions unsupported by evidence
* Recommendations are not required unless a specific development clearly warrants them

CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

News tells the reader what happened.

Polestar should tell the reader:

What changed.

Why it matters.

What it means operationally.

What to watch next.
