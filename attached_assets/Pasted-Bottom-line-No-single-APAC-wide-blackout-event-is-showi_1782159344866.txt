Bottom line

No single APAC wide blackout event is showing, but the region is still under energy stress. The main issues this week are tight refined fuel supply, recurring grid alerts in the Philippines, load shedding in Bangladesh, coal supply concerns in Indonesia, planned local outages in Vietnam, storm and heat related power disruption in India, and wider fuel price pressure in Myanmar and the Pacific.

Reportable items

Philippines, Visayas: recurring brownout risk remains the clearest APAC power issue.
The Visayas grid was again placed under yellow alert on 17 June, with local reporting citing a 984.8 MW shortfall linked to plant outages and reduced capacity. Rotational brownouts have reportedly affected households, schools, hospitals, businesses and government offices for more than a month. Cebu business groups are warning of factory disruption, higher operating costs and supply chain impact.

Bangladesh, southwest region: confirmed load shedding due to supply deficit.
WZPDCL reported demand of 827 MW against supply of 642 MW on 16 June, leaving a 185 MW deficit managed through load shedding. This is operationally relevant for manufacturing, cold chain, telecoms, retail and any site dependent on steady grid power.

Indonesia: coal supply to PLN is under government attention after blackout reports.
Indonesia’s energy minister said on 18 June that the government is working to stabilise coal supply to state utility PLN after reports of blackouts. The immediate issue appears to be medium grade coal supply for power generation rather than a nationwide grid failure.

Vietnam, Can Tho: planned power cuts through 22 to 28 June.
Multiple districts in Can Tho have scheduled outages for maintenance and low and medium voltage works, with some cuts lasting most of the working day. This is not a crisis indicator by itself, but it is worth flagging for local business continuity, cold storage, workshops, clinics and small manufacturing.

India: heat and storms are driving localised power disruption.
North India is seeing record electricity demand in places such as Prayagraj, while Haryana reported extensive weather damage to electricity poles and transformers. Chennai also saw outages after heavy winds damaged parts of the local power network, including disruption affecting cold storage facilities in Koyambedu.

Myanmar: fuel shock is now an economic and logistics pressure point.
The World Bank cut Myanmar’s 2026/27 growth forecast, citing the global energy shock as a driver of higher transport, logistics, production and distribution costs. This matters for supply chains, humanitarian access, generator fuel, road movement and local operating costs.

Regional energy picture

Asia’s crude imports are recovering, but refined products remain tight. Reuters reports expected June crude imports of 22.18 million bpd, up from May, but still below the pre conflict average. Refined fuel exports are also improving but remain around 13% below pre conflict levels. That means fuel availability may improve unevenly, while prices and diesel availability remain exposed.

Southeast Asia remains structurally exposed because of its reliance on oil and gas moving through the Strait of Hormuz. The IEA assessment published this week says the Iran war has forced a regional reassessment of energy security, with higher bills, inflation pressure, and renewed reliance on coal during crisis periods.
