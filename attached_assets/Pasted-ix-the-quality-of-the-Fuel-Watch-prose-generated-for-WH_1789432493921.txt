WHAT MATTERS

Produce 2 to 4 concise analytical paragraphs. Identify the most important developments, where the exposure sits, and the direction of travel. Prioritise material developments rather than summarising every incident.

IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS

This section must describe business consequences, not issue instructions.

Do not write:

Review...
Check...
Consider...
Revisit...
Test...

Explain what the developments mean for:

delivered fuel cost
transport and logistics cost
aviation surcharges where relevant
supplier pricing
replenishment times
stock resilience
operational continuity
routing and landed cost

Write one coherent analytical paragraph of approximately 70 to 120 words.

POLESTAR VIEW

Provide the assessment, not a summary.

State the current risk level using Polestar terminology and explain why.

Distinguish between:

price pressure
physical supply constraint
refinery or terminal disruption
distribution constraint
routing or chokepoint exposure

Identify the principal business exposure and near term direction.

Do not simply repeat What Matters or Implications for Business.

Approximately 70 to 120 words.

WATCH NEXT

Write a forward looking analytical paragraph, not a list of generic monitoring instructions.

Do not repeatedly use:

Watch for...

Identify the specific indicators that would materially change the assessment, such as refinery restart dates, terminal throughput, allocation changes, shortages, delivery times, vessel rerouting, chokepoint restrictions, government action or significant benchmark movements.

Explain what those indicators would mean.

Approximately 50 to 90 words.

STYLE

Concise professional prose.

No filler.

No generic risk language.

No repetitive sentence structures.

No recommendations disguised as implications.

No invented facts.

Every factual statement must remain grounded in the Fuel Watch evidence set.

Analytical conclusions may be derived from validated evidence, but must not introduce new factual claims.
