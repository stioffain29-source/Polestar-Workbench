Shipping Watch and Cargo Watch need another tightening pass. Do not touch Fuel Watch.

First: revert/confirm severity colours.

Brand colours and severity colours are separate.

Brand colours:
- Midnight Blue: #0b0a3d
- Electric Blue: #465bff
- Dusk Gray: #363636
- Polar Gray: #e2e2e2

Severity colours:
- Extreme: #800000
- High: #C0392B
- Moderate: #E67E22
- Low: #6FB872
- Insignificant: #B8C2CC

Do not replace severity colours with brand colours.

Shipping Watch fixes:

1. Reduce table dominance
The Vessel Attacks table is too long and dominates the report.
Show only the most relevant records, not every duplicate/semi-duplicate item.
Prioritise validated maritime security, vessel attack, seizure, piracy, port disruption and chokepoint records.
Demote duplicate syndicated versions of the same event.

2. Improve Chokepoint Watch quality
Operational Read should not be led by weak/noisy headlines.
Do not use repatriation stories, social media-style items, speculative strike claims or generic commentary as lead operational reads.
Use credible maritime/security/industry/advisory records.
If the available data is weak, say:
“Low-confidence reporting only; no validated maritime advisory or credible operational incident identified.”

3. Strengthen written analysis
Shipping needs Fuel Watch-level substance.
Expand:
- What Matters
- Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View

Make them specific to:
- routing
- port calls
- insurance and war-risk premium
- vessel scheduling
- bunker planning
- cargo flow
- operator advisories
- crew-change and routing decisions

4. Tighten Commercial Impact
Do not let freight market commentary dominate.
Commercial Impact should focus on operational consequence:
- freight cost linked to disruption
- insurance premium movement
- port disruption
- route diversion
- schedule reliability
- cargo flow impact

5. Fix final page waste
Do not leave Source Notes / Disclaimer sitting alone on a mostly blank page if it can be pulled back.

Cargo Watch fixes:

1. Add more written value
Cargo needs more analysis, not just a short cargo incident digest.
Expand:
- What Matters
- Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View

Make them specific to:
- inventory loss
- fulfilment impact
- insurance exposure
- repeat corridors
- warehouse/depot controls
- driver and yard staff vetting
- route security
- handover/seal integrity
- high-value cargo moves

2. Tighten Related Incidents
Remove weak/generic duplicate rows such as:
- “Warehouse theft - Electronics” repeated
- “Container theft - Other”
- “Warehouse theft - Other”

Prefer records with:
- named place
- named corridor
- clear cargo type
- clear modus operandi
- clear operational relevance

3. Expand Watch Next
Cargo Watch Next should include:
- repeat losses on same corridor
- copycat theft after publicised incident
- insider involvement
- depot access failures
- seal/lock failures
- insurance or police alerts
- route displacement to weaker depots

4. Fix final page waste
Do not leave Source Notes / Disclaimer sitting alone on a mostly blank page.

Acceptance:
- Shipping no longer feels table-dominated.
- Cargo has more written analytical value.
- Related Incidents are stricter.
- What Matters / Implications / Watch Next / Polestar View are closer to Fuel Watch quality.
- Severity colours are the proper severity palette, not brand colours.
- No dead final disclaimer page if avoidable.
