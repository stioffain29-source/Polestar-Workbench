Apply the final Fuel Watch report standard to Shipping Watch and Cargo Watch.

Scope:
- Shipping Watch
- Cargo Watch

Do not change Fuel Watch. Treat Fuel Watch as the working benchmark.

Required approach:
Use the same product logic that now works in Fuel Watch:
- useful Fast Facts
- supporting charts
- written analytical sections
- Market / Operational Read style prose where relevant
- stronger What Matters
- practical Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View
- tighter Related Incidents
- no dashboard filler
- no orphaned headings
- preview and PDF parity

Do not rebuild the plumbing unless required. This is mostly structure, prose and report-quality alignment.

1. Shipping Watch

Shipping Watch should read like a maritime operational risk report, not an incident list.

Use sections:

- Executive Summary
- Fast Facts
- Chokepoint / Route Read
- Vessel Threat and Piracy Read
- Commercial Impact on Shipping
- Regional and Country View
- What Matters
- Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View
- Related Incidents
- Source Notes / Data Notes
- Disclaimer

Fast Facts should include meaningful shipping indicators, for example:
- Reporting Period
- Records in Window
- Highest Severity
- Main Affected Chokepoint
- Vessel Attacks / Seizures 30d
- Piracy / Armed Robbery 30d
- Latest Significant Incident

Keep existing Shipping fixes:
- one Fast Facts section only
- no duplicate Key Metrics section
- no “Unclassified maritime record” in Issue Type Breakdown
- no fake “Top 12” label unless 12 are shown
- no “Location not identified” as a country
- 30-day lookback for Chokepoint Watch, Vessel Attacks, Piracy and Armed Robbery
- no FAO food price story as a lead Shipping incident
- no broken country strings like “South Korea; Iran”
- improved chart bar styling
- correct brand colours

Shipping prose should cover:
- chokepoint exposure
- vessel attacks / seizures
- piracy and armed robbery
- route diversion
- port disruption
- insurance and freight pressure
- cargo flow
- business impact on routing, cost, delay and continuity

2. Cargo Watch

Cargo Watch should read like a cargo-security and logistics-risk report, not a generic incident digest.

Use sections:

- Executive Summary
- Fast Facts
- Cargo Security Read
- Logistics and Node Exposure
- Regional and Country View
- What Matters
- Implications for Business
- Watch Next
- Polestar View
- Related Incidents
- Source Notes / Data Notes
- Disclaimer

Fast Facts should include meaningful cargo indicators, for example:
- Reporting Period
- Records in Window
- Highest Severity
- Main Issue Type
- Most Affected Country / Area
- Theft / Pilferage Records
- Warehouse / Logistics Node Disruption
- Latest Significant Incident

Cargo Watch prose should cover:
- theft and pilferage
- cargo loss
- warehouse disruption
- trucking and route exposure
- port / logistics node disruption
- labour or access disruption affecting cargo movement
- supply chain loss
- business impact on inventory, fulfilment, insurance and continuity

Important:
Theft and pilferage belong in Cargo Watch.

3. Prose standard for both

Apply the final Fuel Watch prose standard.

Do not use:
- “X records sit...”
- “Activity concentrates...”
- “The usable signal is...”
- “Most recent...”
- “Detail sits...”
- “The reporting window is noisy...”
- “The leading patterns are...”

Use analyst prose:
- explain what the data means
- connect incidents to business risk
- avoid repeated sentence structures
- do not overclaim
- keep it practical and operational

The report should feel like:
- 40 percent data / charts / tables
- 60 percent written analysis

Not a glorified infographic.

4. Related Incidents

Tighten Related Incidents for both reports.

Shipping:
Prioritise:
- vessel attacks
- vessel seizures
- piracy / armed robbery
- port disruption
- route diversion
- chokepoint disruption
- insurance / freight pressure with operational relevance

Exclude or demote:
- generic maritime commentary
- unrelated commodity stories
- shipping finance
- vessel sale / purchase
- newbuild stories
- company earnings

Cargo:
Prioritise:
- cargo theft
- pilferage
- warehouse disruption
- truck hijack
- port / logistics node disruption
- supply chain disruption
- cargo-related protest or strike impacts

Exclude or demote:
- generic trade commentary
- market forecasts
- company finance
- items with no cargo movement or loss relevance

5. Layout and pagination

Apply Fuel Watch final layout discipline:
- no orphaned section headings
- section heading and first paragraph must stay together
- no dead final page with only disclaimer
- Related Incidents should not dominate the report
- Source Notes and Disclaimer should sit cleanly at the end
- preview and PDF must match
