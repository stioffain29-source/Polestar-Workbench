IMPORTANT – ENERGY WATCH MUST USE FLEXIBLE PAGINATION

Do NOT hardcode Energy Watch to five pages.

The current report happens to fit well at approximately five pages, but future reporting periods may contain substantially more or less material.

The Energy Watch template must therefore be CONTENT-DRIVEN rather than PAGE-NUMBER-DRIVEN.

Lock the SECTION ORDER and VISUAL HIERARCHY.

Do not lock sections permanently to specific numbered pages.


REQUIRED SECTION ORDER

The Energy Watch report must always follow this sequence:

1. COVER

2. FAST FACTS

3. BLUF

4. ENERGY SITUATION MAP

5. MARKET PRICES

6. ENERGY SITUATION

7. CURRENT GEOGRAPHIC / ISSUE SECTIONS

8. WHAT MATTERS

9. IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS

10. WATCH NEXT

11. POLESTAR VIEW

12. DISCLAIMER


RELATED INCIDENTS

Do not include the Related Incidents table in the client-facing Energy Watch PDF.

Keep the underlying incident records in Workbench only.


PAGINATION BEHAVIOUR

Allow the report to expand or contract naturally according to the amount of substantive material in that reporting period.

Do not force the report to remain five pages.

If more analysis is required, allow additional pages.

If less analysis is required, do not create unnecessary blank pages.


KEEP SECTIONS TOGETHER WHERE PRACTICAL

Use intelligent page-break controls.

Do not allow:

A heading to appear at the bottom of one page with its content starting on the next page.

A short assessment block to be split unnecessarily across two pages.

Polestar View to split across pages.

The disclaimer heading to appear separately from the disclaimer text.

Fast Facts cards to split across pages.

Individual Market Price cards to split across pages.


MARKET PRICES

Market Prices should be treated as a coherent visual section.

If the complete Market Prices section fits comfortably in the remaining page space, it may remain there.

If it does not fit comfortably, move the whole Market Prices section to the next page.

Do not force unrelated narrative underneath the Market Prices section merely to fill white space.

Do not force Market Prices permanently onto “Page 3”.


ENERGY SITUATION

Energy Situation may use one or more pages depending on the amount of significant reporting.

Generate geographic or issue-based subsections dynamically from the current reporting period.

Do not hardcode countries.

Do not restrict the analysis simply to keep the report within a fixed page count.

The most important locations or issues should receive the greatest analytical space.


WHAT MATTERS

What Matters should follow the Energy Situation analysis.

Keep the section together where reasonably possible.

If there is not enough room for the complete section, begin it on the next page rather than leaving one paragraph stranded on another page.


IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS

Follow What Matters.

Allow the section to flow naturally according to the number of material recommendations.

Do not truncate recommendations to meet a fixed page target.


WATCH NEXT

Follow Implications for Business.

Populate dynamically from the current reporting period.

Keep the section together where practical.


POLESTAR VIEW

Polestar View must remain the final analytical section of the report.

Keep it together as one assessment block.

Do not split it across pages.

Do not force it onto a specific numbered page.


DISCLAIMER

The disclaimer must always follow Polestar View.

Keep the existing wording unchanged.

Use smaller text than the main report body.

Use the normal white page background.

Do not place it in a grey panel, coloured box or shaded area.

Keep the heading and disclaimer body together.

If there is room beneath Polestar View, place it there.

If there is genuinely insufficient room, allow it to continue naturally without distorting the preceding analytical content.


OVERFLOW PRIORITY

When a page is close to overflowing, use this order of adjustment:

1. Remove excessive vertical whitespace.
2. Reduce unnecessary section spacing.
3. Reduce excessive card padding.
4. Move the next complete section to the following page.
5. Add another report page if substantive content genuinely requires it.

Do NOT:

Shrink body text to an unreadable size.

Delete substantive analysis.

Cut recommendations.

Compress charts excessively.

Force sections into unsuitable page space.

Create awkward section splits merely to achieve a predetermined page count.


DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Energy Watch should have a consistent INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE but flexible pagination.

The reader journey must always remain:

SNAPSHOT
Fast Facts
BLUF
Map

MARKETS
Market Prices

ANALYSIS
Energy Situation
Geographic / issue analysis
What Matters

ACTION
Implications for Business
Watch Next

JUDGEMENT
Polestar View

LEGAL
Disclaimer


The number of physical pages may vary from issue to issue.

A quiet reporting period may produce a shorter report.

A significant reporting period may produce a longer report.

That variation is intentional.

Do not sacrifice useful intelligence merely to achieve a fixed page count.

Implement these rules in the shared Energy Watch PDF renderer so they apply to ALL future Energy Watch reports.

Do not hardcode:

Page numbers
Countries
Incident counts
Current reporting-period content
Current Fast Facts
Current narrative

Preserve the section hierarchy while allowing pagination to respond dynamically to the amount of material being reported.
