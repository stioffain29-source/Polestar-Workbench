TASK: REDESIGN CARGO WATCH TO DISPLAY PATTERNS RATHER THAN LONG INCIDENT LISTS

OBJECTIVE

Redesign the Cargo Watch PDF report so that the main report presents patterns, concentrations, operational exposure and business implications rather than several pages of individual incident records.

This is a targeted change to Cargo Watch only.

Do not rebuild the reporting system.
Do not redesign other Watch products.
Do not change the global report template.
Do not refactor unrelated code.
Do not introduce a database migration unless it is genuinely required and approved.

Keep the existing:

1. Cover design
2. Polestar branding
3. Roboto font
4. Header and footer
5. Fast Facts cards
6. Country map
7. Weekly trend chart
8. Existing five tier risk terminology
9. Existing PDF export process


CURRENT PROBLEM

The report begins with useful summary information, mapping and trend analysis, but then turns into several pages of incident rows.

Many incidents are then repeated under a separate Related Incidents section.

This makes the report feel like a printed incident database rather than an intelligence product.

The report needs to move from:

“22 separate incident stories”

to:

“Three or four operational patterns supported by 22 incidents”


STAGE ONE: INSPECT BEFORE EDITING

Before changing code, identify:

1. The files responsible for the Cargo Watch report layout.
2. The files responsible for charts and maps.
3. The Cargo Watch incident data structure.
4. The existing incident deduplication logic.
5. The unique identifier used for incidents.
6. Where category, country, severity, confidence, event date, location and coordinates are stored.
7. Whether graphics use a chart library, SVG, HTML, canvas or another method.
8. Which report sections are template driven.
9. Which report sections use AI generated text.
10. Whether the HTML preview and PDF export use the same rendering path.

Provide a short implementation plan before making edits.


REVISED REPORT STRUCTURE


PAGE 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND FAST FACTS

Retain:

1. Executive Summary
2. Reporting period
3. Total records
4. Highest severity
5. Top issue type
6. Most affected country
7. Latest incident

The Executive Summary should explain:

1. The main cargo exposure.
2. The main affected geography.
3. The most important change during the period.
4. The confidence or limitations in the available reporting.

Do not use vague wording such as:

“There is little to go on this month.”

Use wording such as:

“Reporting remains limited and should be treated as indicative rather than comprehensive.”


PAGE 2: GEOGRAPHIC AND TIME PATTERN

Retain:

1. Country incident map
2. Weekly Cargo Theft Trend chart

Add one short analytical statement below each graphic.

Examples:

“Reporting was concentrated in Indonesia, although this may partly reflect stronger local media coverage.”

“Activity increased during the final reporting week, driven mainly by warehouse and inland transport incidents.”

These statements must be generated from the actual report data.

Do not hardcode country names, dates or categories.


PAGE 3: SUPPLY CHAIN EXPOSURE

Replace detailed incident listings with a horizontal supply chain exposure graphic.

Use these stages:

1. Warehouse and depot
2. Inland transport
3. Staging yard
4. Port and terminal
5. Maritime
6. Cross cutting or enforcement activity

For each stage show:

1. Number of unique incidents
2. Percentage of the total
3. Highest severity
4. Main affected country
5. Primary control concern

Example presentation:

Warehouse and Depot
9 incidents
41 percent of reporting
Primary concern: access control and after hours security

Inland Transport
4 incidents
18 percent of reporting
Primary concern: route security and driver integrity

Port and Terminal
2 incidents
9 percent of reporting
Primary concern: access control and cargo custody

This must be presented as connected cards or a supply chain graphic.

Do not use another standard table.


CATEGORY MAPPING

Create a configurable category mapping.

Do not hardcode the mapping directly inside the PDF component.

Arrest of cargo crime group
Cargo recovery

Enforcement records must not be counted as cargo losses unless the source incident also describes a confirmed loss.


PAGE 4: PATTERN DASHBOARD

Create up to four analytical pattern cards.

Cards must be selected dynamically based on the most significant grouped patterns during the reporting period.

Each card must show:

1. Pattern name
2. Number of unique incidents
3. Share of total reporting
4. Primary geography
5. Highest severity
6. Operational concern
7. Control affected
8. Watch next indicator

Example:

WAREHOUSE INTRUSION

9 incidents

Primary geography: Indonesia

Operational concern:
Repeated access failures and after hours intrusion.

Control affected:
Perimeter security, CCTV, guarding and access control.

Watch next:
Repeat incidents involving the same location, operator or method.

Do not create empty cards.

Where fewer than four meaningful patterns exist, show two or three cards.

Do not fill the page with weak or irrelevant categories simply to maintain a fixed number of cards.


PAGE 5: INCIDENT TIMELINE

Create a horizontal timeline grouped into category lanes.

Suggested lanes:

1. Warehouse and depot
2. Inland transport
3. Port and terminal
4. Maritime
5. Enforcement activity

Each incident should appear as a small marker on the date it occurred.

Marker information should be limited to:

1. Date
2. Short location
3. Category
4. Severity

The timeline is intended to show clustering and changes in activity.

Do not reproduce full news headlines on the timeline.

Use the existing chart library where practical.

Where no suitable chart component exists, use lightweight SVG.

Do not add a large new chart dependency solely for this graphic.


PAGE 6: PRIORITY MATRIX

Create a two by two pattern matrix.

Horizontal axis:
Frequency

Vertical axis:
Operational consequence

Plot grouped patterns rather than individual incidents.

Use these quadrants:

1. Monitor
2. Emerging Concern
3. Persistent Exposure
4. Priority Action

Frequency must be based on the number of unique incidents.

Operational consequence must use a documented and configurable scoring model.

The score may consider:

1. Severity
2. Violence or weapon involvement
3. High value cargo involvement
4. Organised crime indicators
5. Insider involvement indicators
6. Business interruption potential
7. Confirmed cargo loss
8. Repeat activity on the same route or facility

The scoring rules must be visible in configuration or clearly documented in code comments.

Do not ask an AI model to silently invent a score.

Where the data is too limited to support a meaningful matrix, display:

“Insufficient reporting for a meaningful priority comparison this period.”


PAGE 7: OPERATIONAL ASSESSMENT

Consolidate the current narrative sections into:

1. Situation
2. What Matters
3. Business Priorities
4. Watch Next
5. Polestar View

SITUATION

Use no more than two short paragraphs.

Describe the current exposure and where activity is concentrated.

WHAT MATTERS

Present three concise analytical findings.

BUSINESS PRIORITIES

Present no more than five recommended actions.

Each action must be connected to a pattern identified in the report.

WATCH NEXT

Present no more than six forward indicators.

POLestar VIEW

Provide a clear assessment rather than repeating incident descriptions.

Do not state that organised crime, insider involvement or route targeting is confirmed unless the underlying records support that conclusion.

Where evidence is limited, use language such as:

“The available reporting is consistent with possible route familiarity.”

Do not present an inference as a confirmed fact.


FINAL SECTION: CONDENSED INCIDENT APPENDIX

Keep the complete incident register only as an appendix.

Use one row per unique incident.

Columns:

1. Date
2. Location
3. Category
4. Incident summary
5. Severity
6. Confidence

Requirements:

1. One sentence maximum for each incident.
2. Do not reproduce long news headlines.
3. Do not repeat the same incident in different sections.
4. Remove the separate Related Incidents section.
5. Do not print source count unless there is a specific analytical reason.
6. Do not print “Vessel: not reported”.
7. Do not print “Company: not reported”.
8. Do not print “Time: not reported”.
9. Do not repeatedly print “Location not reported”.
10. Leave missing fields blank where appropriate.
11. Use “Unconfirmed” only where analytically relevant.
12. Keep the appendix to no more than two pages under normal reporting volumes.

The same event must not appear once under land side incidents and again under port related incidents unless they are genuinely separate events.


DEDUPLICATION REQUIREMENTS

All calculations and displays must use the same deduplicated incident set.

Use the existing unique incident ID where reliable.

Where separate news reports describe the same event, deduplicate using a combination of:

1. Normalised title
2. Event date
3. Location
4. Category
5. Named company, asset or organisation
6. Cargo type
7. Source similarity

Do not deduplicate only by exact headline.

Different publishers frequently use different headlines for the same incident.

The following totals must reconcile exactly:

1. Total Records card
2. Country map
3. Weekly trend
4. Supply chain exposure graphic
5. Pattern dashboard
6. Timeline
7. Priority matrix
8. Incident appendix

An incident may contribute to a country, category and supply chain stage, but it must only count once in the total number of records.


SPARSE DATA BEHAVIOUR

The report must work correctly when there are:

1. No incidents
2. One incident
3. Fewer than five incidents
4. No named country
5. No named port
6. No usable coordinates
7. Only one category
8. Only one severity level
9. No meaningful comparison between patterns

Do not create large empty report sections.

NO NAMED PORTS

Omit the Named Port Breakdown section completely.

NO MEANINGFUL COUNTRY COMPARISON

Replace the map with:

“Insufficient location data for a meaningful geographic comparison.”

NO MEANINGFUL TIMELINE

Show a compact chronological strip rather than an empty chart.

NO INCIDENTS

Generate a short report stating that no qualifying incidents were identified during the reporting period.

Do not generate empty charts, blank cards or empty tables.


DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

1. Maintain the existing Polestar colour palette.
2. Maintain Roboto throughout.
3. Keep the report light and clean.
4. Do not introduce charcoal or taupe backgrounds.
5. Do not reduce text to an unreadable size to force content onto a page.
6. Avoid dense tables in the main report.
7. Ensure all graphics render correctly in the exported PDF.
8. Avoid page breaks through cards, graphics or table rows.
9. Keep severity colours consistent with the existing five tier terminology.
10. Do not use decorative graphics that do not communicate data.
11. Do not change other Watch reports.
12. Do not modify the shared PDF layout unless necessary for Cargo Watch and confirmed safe for other reports.
