Conflict developments across Southeast Asia last week were dominated by a marked escalation in lethal violence in Myanmar and Indonesia's Papua, even as the Philippines saw the assassination of a ranking Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) commander and fresh killings in Negros Occidental claimed by the communist insurgent group, the New People's Army (NPA). Myanmar's regime was accused of slaying over 40 civilians near Bagan in what analysts described as the latest in a string of massacres carried out under its rebranded civilian administration. In Indonesia's Papua, the death toll from a shooting in Kembru rose to 12, including a child, whilst a separatist group claimed a teenager was killed in a military drone strike. Indonesia and Malaysia were separately drawn into transnational terrorism investigations after a Palestinian man arrested in Greece confessed to Hamas membership and training in Indonesia and Malaysia, whilst a Cyprus-based terror suspect's confession revealed he had met a Hamas operative in Malaysia. The Thailand–Cambodia border dispute deepened further as both sides traded accusations over the UNCLOS conciliation process, and in the West Philippine Sea, China sanctioned the Philippine defence secretary over his remarks on a disputed floating structure at Bajo de Masinloc, marking a significant escalation in the diplomatic standoff.

Thailand–Cambodia Border Conflict
The Thailand–Cambodia border dispute showed no sign of easing last week, with both sides trading sharp rhetoric over the UNCLOS conciliation process that Cambodia had invoked. A Thai scholar argued that Thailand failed to understand maritime law, whilst Cambodian analysts criticised Bangkok’s resistance to the conciliation mechanism. Cambodia’ senate president Hun Sen insisted that the border closure remained Thailand’s choice but that Cambodian territory must be respected, and separately rebuked Thai media over what he described as false claims regarding Cambodia’s border stance. Prime minister Hun Manet urged full implementation of the existing border agreement and warned that the fragile ceasefire required urgent action to secure lasting peace. Thailand’s National Security Council assessed that border tension remained manageable even as it monitored reports of Chinese-made tanks on the Cambodian side, and Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul warned against any encroachment on Thai sovereignty following Hun Sen’s remarks. Thailand and Vietnam advanced their bilateral ties, with commentary suggesting Vietnam stood to benefit from the continuing Cambodia–Thailand tensions. A commentary piece warned that prolonged border tensions could ultimately lead to the end of Thai investment in Cambodia, underscoring the economic stakes of the unresolved dispute.

Myanmar
Myanmar’s regime was accused of slaying more than 40 civilians in a rampage near the Bagan temple town, with a new report finding that the killing formed part of a pattern of nine massacres in two months that unmasked the reality behind the junta’s rebranded civilian administration. The Arakan Army stepped up its assault on a regime naval base near Sittwe, and fighting flared as the regime attempted to secure a highway route linking India, Myanmar, and Thailand. Civilians evacuated the town of Shwegu as regime forces pushed into Kachin State, where a new report found that 15 years of renewed warfare had killed 3,000 civilians, a grim milestone marked by Kachin communities demanding an end to a China-backed dam project and a halt to the fighting. Myanmar’s UN ambassador criticised the Security Council for what were described as failures amid the escalating military attacks. Internationally, a US scholar with a history of activism in Myanmar was arrested in China on suspicion of espionage, and Indonesia warned the Myanmar regime that the Asean peace plan remained crucial, even as a rights group reported that exposed regime military suppliers continued to operate in Singapore.

Vietnam
Vietnam was reported to have purchased Indian cruise missiles, a move analysts linked to growing wariness of China’s military buildup in the region. Separately, an analysis examined Vietnam’s ongoing construction boom in the disputed Spratly Islands chain, pointing to Hanoi’s own steady expansion of its presence in contested waters even as it called for restraint from other parties.

Malaysia
Malaysia featured prominently in the unfolding investigation into an alleged Hamas operative arrested in…

…Greece, with multiple reports indicating that the suspect had trained in Malaysia before travelling to Europe, with a cruise ship reportedly among the alleged targets. Meanwhile, a Cyprus-based terror suspect was reported to have met a Hamas operative in Malaysia, raising questions about the country’s exposure to transnational extremist networks. On a different front, Japan and Malaysia agreed to boost cooperation on energy and maritime security, whilst an analysis examined the potential impact of a Taiwan Strait crisis on the Malaysian economy.

Thailand
Three people were injured in a roadside bombing outside a school in Yala, the latest suspected insurgent attack to target civilian infrastructure in the Deep South. A Thai court sentenced two Chinese Uyghur men to death for the 2015 Bangkok shrine bombing, a verdict that drew condemnation from a Uyghur human rights activist. On the peace process front, a Thai member of parliament urged public support for southern insurgency talks planned in Malaysia, and the newly appointed southern peace negotiator promised to approach the role with an open mind. Commentary continued to highlight the pitfalls on the path to peace in the Deep South, reflecting persistent skepticism about the prospects for a durable settlement despite the renewed dialogue track.

Philippines
A ranking MILF commander was shot dead in Maguindanao del Sur, a significant killing that raised fresh questions about internal discipline and rivalries within the organisation following its recent suspension of its military chief. The NPA claimed responsibility for the killing of a village peacemaker and a farmer in Binalbagan and Calatrava, prompting condemnation from the government’s anti-communist insurgency task force, which denounced what it described as the NPA’s “spy-tagging execution” practices. Police and NPA forces clashed in Negros Occidental, and a cop was injured in a Maguindanao del Sur ambush allegedly carried out by a terror group. A drug suspect linked to a terror group was killed in a shootout with police in Lanao del Sur, and the army continued operations against remaining Dawlah Islamiyah elements in Maguindanao del Sur, seizing firearms in the process.

On the peace consolidation front, a Samar town was declared insurgency-free after decades of NPA influence, Agusan del Sur was assessed as under stable peace and security status, and Calabarzon was declared insurgency-free on Independence Day. The Office of the Presidential Adviser on Peace, Reconciliation and Unity or OPAPRU signalled a shift toward peace sustainability as Caraga approached being declared free of communist rebels. The Bangsamoro interim chief urged unity following the MILF’s recent suspension of his military leadership position whilst standing firm on accountability, as preparations continued for the BARMM election and MILF decommissioning process.

In the West Philippine Sea, tensions escalated sharply after China was found to have installed a floating structure at Bajo de Masinloc, which the Philippines linked to illegal marine research and possible data theft, and urged Beijing to remove. China responded by sanctioning Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro over what it described as erroneous remarks, a move Manila protested as an unfriendly act whilst Teodoro remained defiant. Chinese naval activity surged east of Taiwan amid the broader spat with the Philippines and Japan, and the second Japan–US–Philippines maritime dialogue took place against this backdrop, with the three countries seeking to align their trilateral foreign aid efforts. The Philippine military reported that Chinese vessel presence in the West Philippine Sea had dipped slightly to 41 from 44 the previous week, even as the floating structure at Scarborough Shoal was observed shifting position, renewing concern over Beijing’s intentions in the area.

Indonesia
Violence in Papua intensified further last week, with the death toll from a shooting in Kembru rising to 12, including a child. A separatist group claimed that a teenager was killed in a military drone bomb attack, whilst the Indonesian military reported that it had repelled separatists from three villages in South Papua and evacuated 44 gold miners from a separatist disturbance in the Papua Mountains. The army stated that separatists had burned down 10 school buildings in Papua over the past two years, and a man was arrested for allegedly supplying weapons to the separatist group. In a notable development, eight Papuan separatists pledged allegiance to the Republic of Indonesia, handing over their flag to the military. Separately, a Palestinian man arrested in Greece confessed to Hamas membership and was linked to Indonesia and Malaysia.

Region
At the regional level, the United States confirmed it was monitoring Chinese activity in the South China Sea around the disputed shoal at the centre of the Philippines dispute. Russian scholars proposed measures to strengthen trust in addressing the South China Sea issue, whilst China’s Coast Guard conducted what it described as a rights-protection patrol near the Diaoyu Islands aka Senkaku Islands in Japan aka Diaoyutai Islands in Taiwan, extending Beijing’s assertive maritime posture beyond Southeast Asia. A Vietnamese diplomat stressed that Asean must remain central to regional dialogue and trust-building, even as commentary warned that China’s latest moves in the South China Sea had reopened old wounds and raised fresh questions about the risk of open conflict.
