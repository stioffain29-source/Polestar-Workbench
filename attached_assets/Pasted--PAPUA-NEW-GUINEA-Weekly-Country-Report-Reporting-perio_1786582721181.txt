# PAPUA NEW GUINEA

**Weekly Country Report**
**Reporting period:** 6 to 12 August 2026
**Overall severity:** Moderate

## BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT

Localised armed violence and growing pressure on essential supplies shaped Papua New Guinea’s risk environment. Enga remained the principal security concern following an armed attack on a senior police convoy, while communal fighting in the Duke of York Islands demonstrated how quickly local disputes can escalate. Operationally, shipping disruption, drought, coastal erosion and planned water restrictions threatened food availability, transport and essential services. These issues do not represent a nationwide crisis, but they create significant local risks to staff movement and business continuity.

## TOP 3 DEVELOPMENTS

### ARMED AMBUSH TARGETS ENGA POLICE COMMANDER

**Severity:** High
**Location:** Yaltep, Enga Province
**Occurrence date:** Not disclosed; the incident had occurred by 6 August

Gunmen opened fire on Enga Provincial Police Commander Chief Superintendent Steven Harris and his convoy while officers were travelling to arrest suspects linked to a murder investigation. Three police reservists returned fire and PNGDF personnel secured the area, allowing the convoy to continue. No police casualties were reported.

### SHIPPING DISRUPTION CAUSES SHORTAGES IN VANIMO

**Severity:** Moderate
**Location:** Vanimo, West Sepik Province
**Occurrence date:** Ongoing; conditions confirmed as at 12 August

Approximately three months of irregular shipping left shops in Vanimo short of food and other essential goods. Between 30 and 40 containers were reportedly awaiting shipment from Lae, with no confirmed date for normal services to resume.

### EIGHTY ARRESTED AFTER DUKE OF YORK CLASH

**Severity:** Moderate
**Location:** Duke of York Islands, East New Britain Province
**Occurrence date:** Late in the week ending 9 August; exact date not disclosed

Three villages were involved in a community clash that resulted in two houses being burned. Police arrested 80 people, all of whom were later released on bail pending court proceedings. No deaths or serious injuries were reported.

## CURRENT SITUATION

Risk remained uneven and location specific. Enga presented the main security concern because of illegal firearms, armed offenders and continuing police operations, while the Duke of York clash appeared contained following the arrests. There was no evidence that the two incidents were connected or formed part of a wider national pattern. Outside these areas, disruption to food, water and transport presented the more immediate operational challenge.

## OTHER OPERATIONAL DISRUPTION

As at 12 August, shops in Vanimo were short of food and other essential goods after approximately three months of irregular shipping. On 12 August, authorities allocated K1 million for emergency works at Matugar, where coastal erosion was threatening the Madang to Bogia Highway. Drought continued to reduce water availability and food production in Hela. Port Moresby also faced a partial water shutdown from 14 to 16 August for critical pipeline repairs, creating an immediate risk of disruption to businesses and essential services.

## GOVERNANCE & REGULATORY

Policing activity focused on the firearms amnesty and the approaching transition to enforcement. On 11 August, community leaders in Jiwaka voluntarily surrendered six illegal firearms, ammunition and a live grenade under Operation Kumul Peace. The operation’s awareness phase runs until 15 August, followed by a formal surrender period from 16 to 31 August and enforcement action thereafter. The national grace period ends on 16 September, with compulsory operations expected from 17 September. The Jiwaka surrender was not reported as being connected to the Enga ambush.

## CRIME, THEFT & ROBBERY

The incidents in Enga and the Duke of York Islands do not indicate a nationwide increase in raskol activity or organised crime. The Enga ambush demonstrates that armed offenders remain willing to attack official convoys, while the Duke of York clash appears to have been localised communal violence. The surrender of weapons in Jiwaka was a positive development but also highlighted the continued availability of firearms in the Highlands. No major commercial robbery, cargo theft or urban raskol incident was identified during the reporting period.

# ACTIONS & OUTLOOK

## RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

### MOVEMENT

* Confirm conditions before travelling through Enga, particularly near Yaltep and Sirunki.
* Defer non essential movement during active police or PNGDF operations.
* Check the Matugar section of the Madang to Bogia Highway before travel and use the route during daylight.
* Confirm food, fuel and accommodation availability before deploying personnel to Vanimo.

### SITE SECURITY

* Review access controls and guard instructions at sites in Enga and areas affected by community disputes.
* Direct guards to report armed activity or police deployments immediately and avoid intervening in confrontations.
* Secure water storage and critical equipment at Port Moresby sites before restrictions begin on 14 August.
* Check stocks of food, fuel and other essential supplies at facilities in Vanimo and Hela.

### STAFF AWARENESS

* Brief staff in Enga to avoid police operations, armed groups and gatherings linked to local disputes.
* Require immediate reporting of roadblocks, gunfire, community fighting or sudden police deployments.
* Advise staff in Port Moresby, Vanimo and Hela of expected water or supply constraints.

### ESCALATION

Escalate if armed violence affects a staff route or business location, police operations restrict movement, shortages threaten essential supplies, the Madang to Bogia Highway becomes impassable at Matugar, or Port Moresby’s water restrictions continue beyond 16 August.

## OUTLOOK: NEXT SEVEN DAYS

Operational disruption is likely to remain the main concern. Port Moresby’s water restrictions may disrupt workplaces and essential services, while shortages in Vanimo and drought related pressures in Hela are unlikely to ease quickly. Police and PNGDF operations are expected to continue in Enga following the ambush, increasing the possibility of checkpoints, arrests and short notice movement restrictions. Jiwaka’s formal firearms surrender period begins on 16 August and may produce further weapons handovers. Renewed violence in the Duke of York Islands remains possible but is less likely while police maintain oversight of the affected communities.

## ESCALATION INDICATORS

* Further armed attacks on police, PNGDF personnel or official convoys in Enga.
* Renewed fighting, arson or mobilisation among the affected Duke of York communities.
* Water restrictions in Port Moresby continuing beyond 16 August or disrupting essential services.
* Worsening shortages in Vanimo, closure of the Madang to Bogia Highway at Matugar, or further drought deterioration in Hela.

# POLESTAR VIEW

Papua New Guinea is not facing a single nationwide security crisis, but this week’s events show how quickly localised violence and weak infrastructure can disrupt operations. The Enga ambush demonstrates that state authority remains contested in parts of the Highlands, while the Jiwaka weapons surrender is positive but too limited to reduce the wider firearms threat. For businesses, the more immediate exposure comes from basic service and supply failures, including water restrictions in Port Moresby, shortages in Vanimo, drought in Hela and the threatened highway at Matugar. These conditions require location specific contingency measures rather than countrywide restrictions, with priority given to route verification, essential stocks and water resilience.
