PAGE 3 – CHOKEPOINT WATCH

Replace the current long bullet format with a single clean chokepoint matrix.

Each chokepoint should appear as one horizontal row.

Use the following structure:

CHOKEPOINT NAME | RISK LEVEL | INCIDENTS | MOVEMENT | LATEST INCIDENT

Under each row, include one short operational impact line only.

Example:

STRAIT OF HORMUZ | EXTREME | 2 INCIDENTS | 170 VESSELS TRACKED | LATEST 09 SEP

Operational impact: Route disruption, war risk insurance pressure and increased voyage planning requirements.

Repeat the same structure for:

Strait of Hormuz
Bab el Mandeb
Red Sea
Suez Canal
Gulf of Aden
Singapore Strait
Malacca Strait

Avoid separate boxes for every data point.

Avoid large bullet lists.

The page should allow the reader to scan all seven chokepoints quickly and compare them visually.

PAGE 4 – THREAT PICTURE

Use this page for vessel attacks, seizures, piracy and armed robbery.

Do not present the main threat picture as another large spreadsheet.

Use a timeline or incident sequence for the major events.

Each incident should show:

Date
Location
Incident type
Severity
Short title

Major incidents should carry greater visual weight than minor incidents.

Include a secondary section for piracy and armed robbery beneath or alongside the main threat timeline.

AIS and vessel movement data should be presented as context, not as a separate incident category.

Where useful, include a compact vessel movement graphic for the major routes rather than repeating long movement sentences.

Cargo delivery impact

Use the lower half for visual regional analysis.

Include:

INCIDENTS BY REGION

RECORDS BY COUNTRY

Do not split charts across pages.

PAGE 6 – WHAT MATTERS

This should be the main analytical page.

Use three clear sections:

WHAT MATTERS

This is the main analytical judgement and should explain what the combined maritime picture means.

IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS

Use concise operational implications for clients.

WATCH NEXT

Show the key indicators and developments that should be monitored during the next reporting period.

This page should feel analytical rather than data heavy.

Use strong section hierarchy and more white space than the data pages.

Place the Polestar View at the top of the page in a prominent assessment panel.

DISCLAIMER

Do not use a standalone disclaimer page.

Move the existing disclaimer to the bottom of the final report page.

Use a shallow Polar Gray footer panel.

Use smaller Roboto text.

Retain the existing disclaimer wording unchanged.

The disclaimer should be visually separate from the analytical content but should not consume meaningful page space.

OVERALL DESIGN RULES

Use one consistent interior design language across the whole report.

Use Polestar colours only:

Midnight Blue #0b0a3d
Electric Blue #465bff
Dusk Gray #363636
Polar Gray #e2e2e2

Use Roboto and Roboto Light throughout.

Avoid charcoal and taupe.

Reduce the number of outlined boxes significantly.

Use strong full width bands, clean tables, charts and deliberate spacing instead.

Do not allow sections to break awkwardly across pages.

Do not begin a chart or section on one page and continue it on the next.

Major incidents and major risk areas should carry greater visual weight than quiet routes or low confidence reporting.

Do not give every piece of information equal visual importance.

The report should look like a premium maritime intelligence product, not a database export.

The reader journey should be:

FAST FACTS
BLUF
MAP
CHOKEPOINT PICTURE
THREAT PICTURE
COMMERCIAL IMPACT
REGIONAL PICTURE
WHAT MATTERS
IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS
WATCH NEXT
POLESTAR VIEW
RELATED INCIDENTS
DISCLAIMER

The report should be shorter, cleaner, more visual and easier to scan.
