Report generator improvement notes for Replit

Objective

Improve the Polestar country report generator so the output feels analyst led, customer relevant and credible. This is not a rebuild. It is an enhancement to the existing report generation, incident filtering, deduplication, mapping and narrative logic.

The main issues to fix are:

1. Weak or irrelevant items getting through the incident filter
2. Duplicate reporting of the same incident, including the sandal factory fire
3. Fire incidents being reported without explaining whether they are fire safety, arson, unrest related or unknown cause
4. Map markers being placed at city centre or province centre level, which creates false precision
5. Top 3 Developments sometimes including low value or weakly relevant stories
6. Reports not clearly explaining what matters to business users

Required changes

1. Incident quality gate

Before an item is allowed into the report narrative, Top 3 Developments, map or charts, it must pass a quality gate.

Exclude items where the reporting is only:

General tourism
General economic commentary
Routine politics
Routine court or admin process
Minor crime with no business relevance
Generic social media chatter
Old incidents being recycled as new
Duplicated coverage of an already captured incident
National level commentary with no operational relevance
Opinion pieces with no incident or location attached

An item should only be included where it has at least one of the following:

Clear location
Clear date
Clear incident type
Operational impact
Security relevance
Business continuity relevance
Staff movement relevance
Site, logistics or asset exposure
Police, emergency service or security force response
Road closure, evacuation, fire, protest, violent crime or disruption

If an item does not meet this threshold, exclude it from the customer report. It can remain in raw data if needed, but it should not appear in the report.

2. Deduplication

Add stronger deduplication before report generation.

The same incident must not appear more than once just because it has been reported by several sources.

Deduplicate using:

Date
Location
Named premises
Incident type
Key words
Source title similarity
Known named incident
Reported casualties or damage
Neighbourhood, district or city
Time window

Example rule:

If multiple articles report the same sandal factory fire, the report should treat it as one incident. The system may retain multiple source references internally, but the customer report should show one consolidated incident only.

For duplicate candidates, merge them into one incident record with:

Primary incident title
Best available location
Highest confirmed severity
Most specific confirmed cause
One short summary
All source links retained internally
Confidence level
Last updated timestamp

3. Fire and explosion classification

Do not treat all fire and explosion incidents as the same security issue.

Every fire or explosion record must be assigned one of these cause tags:

Fire safety
Accidental fire
Electrical fault
Industrial or factory fire
Commercial premises fire
Residential fire
Vehicle fire
Wildfire or environmental fire
Explosion
Arson or suspicious fire
Protest or unrest related fire
Attack related fire
Cause not yet reported

Important rule:

Do not imply arson, attack, sabotage or unrest unless the source clearly states this.

If the source does not confirm the cause, use:

Cause not yet reported

Report wording should say:

“Fire incidents were reported, but cause reporting remains inconsistent. Where no cause is confirmed, Polestar treats these as fire safety and continuity issues rather than intentional security incidents.”

4. Fire reporting in the narrative

The report must explain why fires matter.

Separate security relevance from continuity relevance.

Use this logic:

Fire safety or accidental fire
Relevance is life safety, evacuation, site continuity, building access, utilities and insurance.

Industrial or factory fire
Relevance is staff safety, supply chain continuity, production interruption, emergency access and nearby site exposure.

Arson or suspicious fire
Relevance is security, criminal intent, site protection, staff movement and possible repeat targeting.

Protest or unrest related fire
Relevance is civil unrest, movement restrictions, road closures, crowd control and nearby site exposure.

Explosion
Relevance is life safety, evacuation, emergency services response, access control and business continuity.

Cause unknown
Relevance is provisional. Do not overstate. Treat as fire safety and continuity until cause is confirmed.

5. Top 3 Developments selection

Top 3 Developments must not simply be the three highest scoring scraped items.

Top 3 should be selected using analyst value.

Include items where there is:

High severity
Casualties
Evacuation
Major fire
Significant road closure
Protest disruption
Police or security force deployment
Industrial or logistics impact
Airport, port, toll road or transport impact
Incident near a commercial district or operating area
Regulatory action with direct business impact
Clear movement, site or continuity relevance

Do not include low value items unless they have clear business impact.

Examples of weak Top 3 items to avoid:

Minor theft
Routine tourism commentary
General economic reporting
Political commentary without operational effect
A local crime story with no wider relevance
A duplicate of another Top 3 item
A vague social media post with no reliable location or impact

Each Top 3 item must answer:

What happened
Where it happened
Why it matters
What business users should do or watch

6. Top 3 duplicate prevention

The Top 3 section must not include two versions of the same incident.

If two items concern the same incident, merge them into one entry.

The second slot should go to the next most relevant separate incident.

7. Map location confidence

Stop plotting broad administrative locations as precise incident points.

Every incident needs a location confidence level:

Exact location
Street, building, factory, site, port, station, mall, named road or named premises. Plot as a point.

Good local location
District, subdistrict, town, regency or clearly named area. Plot as an approximate area marker or district marker.

City level only
Jakarta, Surabaya, Bandung, Medan, Jayapura etc. Do not plot as a precise point.

Province level only
West Java, East Java, Papua Tengah, Papua Barat Daya etc. Do not plot as a precise point.

Unknown or unclear
Do not plot.

8. Map plotting rule

Do not place a marker in the middle of Jakarta, or any other city, just because the location is only “Jakarta”.

Do not use city centroids or province centroids as incident points unless the report makes clear that the marker is approximate.

Preferred approach:

Plot exact and good local locations only.
Do not plot city level or province level only records.
Include city level and province level records in totals only.
Add a note below the map explaining what is and is not plotted.

Map note wording:

“Only incidents with usable location detail are plotted. City level, province level and unclear locations are included in the totals but are not shown as precise points. Where several incidents share the same mapped location, one marker is shown and coloured by the highest severity recorded.”

9. Map marker clustering

Where several incidents share the same location, show one marker with a count.

The marker colour should reflect the highest severity at that location.

The map tooltip or table should show:

Location
Number of incidents
Highest severity
Main incident types
Location confidence

10. Records versus incidents

Clarify the difference between records and incidents.

A record is a scraped article or source entry.
An incident is a deduplicated event.

Customer facing charts should use deduplicated incidents where possible.

If charts use records, the report must say “records” clearly and explain that multiple records may relate to the same incident.

Preferred wording:

“Charts show deduplicated incidents for the reporting period.”

If deduplication is not yet complete:

“Charts show source records. Some records may relate to the same incident and are subject to deduplication.”

11. Recommended report wording for fires

Replace generic wording like:

“Fire and explosion incidents were reported.”

With:

“Fire incidents were reported during the period, including commercial, industrial and local premises fires. Where cause is not confirmed, these should be treated as fire safety and continuity issues rather than intentional security incidents.”

For Jakarta:

“Fire reporting this week appears primarily relevant to life safety, building access, emergency response and continuity planning. No intentional cause should be inferred unless confirmed by source reporting.”

For Indonesia:

“Fire reporting was geographically broader and included industrial and local premises incidents. Deduplication is required where several sources report the same fire.”

12. Recommended report wording for map limitations

Add this standard note under every map:

“Only incidents with usable location detail are plotted. Broad city, province or unclear locations are included in totals but are not shown as precise points. Map markers should be read as indicative of reported location, not as a complete operating picture.”

13. Recommended report wording for customer relevance

Add a short Customer Relevance section before Recommended Actions.

For Jakarta:

Customer Relevance
Most relevant to office operations, staff commuting, hotel based visitors, drivers, facilities teams and business continuity leads. Main issues this period are localised fire response, utility disruption, wet weather movement disruption and incidental exposure to crime or protest activity.

For Indonesia:

Customer Relevance
Most relevant to multi site operators, logistics teams, manufacturing sites, field travel, warehousing, cash movement and staff movement between cities. Main issues this period are localised fires, protest disruption, regulatory follow on, flood or hazard disruption and violent crime exposure in affected areas.

For Papua:

Customer Relevance
Most relevant to field operations, project sites, secure movement, aviation dependent travel, remote logistics and staff based near affected districts. Main issues are violent crime, security force activity, movement restriction, site exposure and limited response options in higher risk areas.

14. Recommended Actions logic

Recommended actions must be tied to the actual incident picture.

Avoid generic repeated lines.

For Jakarta, actions should include:

Check backup power and generator readiness where outage reporting is relevant.
Confirm building access and lift operation during power disruption.
Monitor flood and heavy rain warnings before staff movement.
Review routes through Central Jakarta before meetings or visitor movement.
Avoid protest locations and government buildings during known mobilisation.
Check emergency access and evacuation arrangements for sites near reported fires.

For Indonesia, actions should include:

Check whether any reported fires are near company sites, suppliers or logistics routes.
Treat unknown cause fires as continuity issues unless intentional cause is confirmed.
Monitor protest areas before intercity or urban movement.
Check drainage, flood exposure and site access in affected provinces.
Review staff movement where police deployments, unrest or violent crime are reported.
Confirm local authority or regulatory follow on where incidents involve permits, inspections or enforcement.

For Papua, actions should include:

Restrict non essential movement in affected districts.
Use secure transport for essential movement.
Avoid predictable travel patterns.
Track security force operations and localised violence.
Review site exposure near police, military or separatist activity.
Confirm air, road and port status before movement.
Ensure staff have clear reporting lines and check in procedures.

15. Polestar View logic

The Polestar View must be more than a summary.

It should include:

Current risk direction
Main driver of risk
Most exposed geography
Most exposed business activity
Most likely next disruption
What would change the assessment
Practical customer judgement

Suggested structure:

“Polestar assesses that operating risk in [location] is [stable / elevated / deteriorating], driven by [main drivers]. The main business exposure is [movement / site access / staff safety / continuity / logistics]. The most exposed areas are [locations]. The most likely disruption over the next seven days is [scenario]. The assessment would worsen if [trigger]. For now, operators should [action].”

16. Reporting Confidence

Reporting confidence should reflect:

Source reliability
Number of sources
Location precision
Deduplication confidence
Cause clarity
Whether the incident is confirmed by official or multiple local sources

Confidence labels:

High confidence
Multiple credible sources, clear location, clear date, low duplication risk.

Moderate confidence
Multiple sources or credible local reporting, but some uncertainty around location, cause or duplication.

Low confidence
Single source, vague location, unclear cause, social media only, or high duplication risk.

Do not use high confidence where the map points are vague or where the cause of major incidents is unclear.

17. Severity logic

Severity should be based on impact, not just keywords.

High or extreme severity should require one or more of:

Fatalities
Multiple injuries
Major evacuation
Major fire or explosion
Confirmed attack
Major protest disruption
Security force deployment with violence
Transport corridor closure
Direct effect on commercial, industrial or logistics operations
Significant business continuity impact

Low severity should be used where:

Minor theft
No confirmed disruption
No casualties
No business impact
Routine local reporting
Weak source detail

18. Source handling

Keep full source detail internally.

Customer report does not need every source, but the system should retain sources for audit and checking.

For each incident record, store:

Source title
Source outlet
Source date
URL
Scrape timestamp
Incident date
Location
Location confidence
Incident type
Cause tag
Severity
Deduplication group
Summary
Customer relevance
Reason included
Reason excluded, if excluded

19. Incident exclusion reasons

When an item is filtered out, tag the reason.

Use standard exclusion tags:

Duplicate
Too vague
No operational relevance
General news
Tourism only
Routine politics
Minor crime
No clear location
No clear date
Source unreliable
Old incident
Opinion or commentary
Not security relevant
Not continuity relevant

This will make it easier to tune the filter later.

20. Acceptance criteria

The fix is acceptable when:

The same fire does not appear more than once in the report.
The sandal factory fire is consolidated into a single incident.
Fires are classified by likely relevance and cause status.
No report implies arson or intentional cause unless confirmed.
Jakarta map no longer drops vague incidents into the city centre.
City level and province level records are counted but not plotted as precise points.
Top 3 Developments contain three separate and operationally relevant issues.
Weak tourism, general politics, routine economy and minor crime items do not appear in Top 3.
Recommended Actions are specific to the incident picture.
Polestar View gives a clear judgement, not just a summary.
Charts make clear whether they show records or deduplicated incidents.
Reporting Confidence reflects location precision and deduplication quality.

21. Do not change

Do not rebuild the report format.
Do not change the visual brand.
Do not change the PDF export flow unless required for these fixes.
Do not remove the existing sections.
Do not add long source lists to customer facing reports.
Do not over engineer this into a full intelligence platform.

22. Priority order

Priority 1
Deduplication and slop filtering.

Priority 2
Location confidence and map plotting rules.

Priority 3
Fire and explosion cause classification.

Priority 4
Top 3 selection logic.

Priority 5
Customer Relevance and sharper Polestar View wording.

Priority 6
Cleaner chart labelling for records versus incidents.

Expected outcome

The reports should feel less like scraped incident summaries and more like a paid operational risk product. The customer should be able to understand what happened, where it matters, whether it is a security issue or continuity issue, what locations are exposed, and what action they should take.
