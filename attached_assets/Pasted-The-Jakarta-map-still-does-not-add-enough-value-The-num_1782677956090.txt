The Jakarta map still does not add enough value.

The numbered dot approach only works when there are a small number of specific incidents with reliable locations. For Jakarta, the report is not really about single incident points. It is about operating exposure across the city: protests, flooding, crime, logistics, airport transfers and commercial district access.

We need to discuss and test better map formats.

Preferred options:

1. Icon based operational exposure map

Use simple icons instead of numbered dots.

Example icons:

Protest / crowd: Central Jakarta
Flood / rain: North Jakarta, Greater Jakarta commuter belt
Crime / public safety: South Jakarta, West Jakarta, transport hubs
Fire / emergency response: dense commercial or residential areas
Port / logistics: North Jakarta
Airport / transfer route: Soekarno Hatta corridor
Road disruption: cross city movement routes

Each area should show the dominant exposure type, not just a number.

2. Risk area map with labels

Use shaded or outlined areas for:

Central Jakarta: protest and government district disruption
South Jakarta: office, embassy and commercial exposure
West Jakarta: congestion, crime and access disruption
North Jakarta: port, flooding and logistics exposure
East Jakarta: road movement and residential disruption
Soekarno Hatta corridor: airport transfer disruption
Greater Jakarta: commuter belt and weather related movement disruption

This should look like a clean report graphic, not a web map screenshot.

3. Corridor and access map

For Jakarta, the most useful map may be an access map rather than an incident map.

Show:

Airport corridor
Central Jakarta government district
Main commercial and hotel areas
Port and logistics exposure
Greater Jakarta commuter routes

This would better support business users planning airport transfers, meetings, site visits and staff movement.

4. Area exposure table instead of a map

If the map cannot be made useful, replace it with a simple area exposure table.

Example columns:

Area
Main exposure
Operational relevance
Action

This may be more useful than a weak map.

Map rule going forward:

Use incident dots only when there are a small number of specific incidents with reliable locations.

Use icons, shaded areas, callouts or an area exposure table when the report is about broader operating conditions.

The map must answer a practical question:

Where should the reader be cautious, and why?

If it cannot answer that, remove it.
