Papua protest and activism scan: last 7 days, 12 to 19 June 2026

Overall picture: activity appears low in visible street numbers but politically sensitive. Reporting from Papua is patchy, so I would not treat the lack of large confirmed rallies as proof of quiet. The main themes are Biak spaceport opposition, anti militarisation activism, customary land rights, displacement advocacy, and local resistance to plantation or strategic projects.

Biak / spaceport issue, 17 June: The clearest current watch item is the Indonesian government’s approval or renewed push around the Biak spaceport project, despite long running local rejection. Concord Review listed this on 17 June as “Govt. approves spaceport project in Papua despite rejection.” The issue is highly sensitive because local communities and church linked actors have previously opposed the project on land, environment and militarisation grounds.

Biak, reported peaceful action: Activist linked reporting circulating this week claimed a peaceful protest in Biak against militarisation and the planned spaceport, reportedly involving church and customary leaders. I would treat this as low to medium confidence unless corroborated by local media, but it fits the established pattern around Biak: opposition to land acquisition, military facilities and strategic projects without full Indigenous consent.

South Papua / Merauke watch item: Land rights activism around the Merauke sugarcane and rice strategic project remains active as a background issue, though I did not find a new large protest inside the 12 to 19 June window. Human Rights Monitor reported that legal efforts and protests by customary landowners have been ongoing against the South Papua strategic project, with concerns over land rights, exclusion from decision making and military involvement.

Sorong, just outside window but still relevant: On 8 June, residents in Sorong Regency reportedly protested against palm oil company PT Inti Kebun Sejahtera, accusing the company of continuing to harvest palm fruit on disputed land in Klalik Village. This falls just outside the strict seven day window, but it remains relevant to the current activism picture because it reflects the same land rights and plantation pressure theme.

Human rights and displacement advocacy: The wider activist environment remains shaped by conflict, displacement and security force concerns. Human Rights Monitor’s June update said more than 122,000 civilians remained internally displaced across multiple regencies as of early June 2026. That is not a protest event, but it is a major driver for church, civil society and Papuan rights advocacy.

Assessment: Moderate activism level, high sensitivity, low visibility. The most operationally relevant areas are Biak, due to the spaceport and military land issues; Merauke / South Papua, due to strategic agricultural projects; and Sorong / Southwest Papua, due to land and palm oil disputes. Near term disruption risk is likely to be localised, but any protest touching militarisation, the Morning Star symbol, Freeport, land acquisition or self determination can draw a quick security response.
