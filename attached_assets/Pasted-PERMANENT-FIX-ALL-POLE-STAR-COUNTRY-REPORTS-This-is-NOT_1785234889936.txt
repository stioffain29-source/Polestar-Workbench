5. COUNTRY ATTRIBUTION MUST USE PHYSICAL LOCATION
==================================================

The country report must be based primarily on where the event physically happened.

Do not assign an event to a country merely because that country is mentioned.

Examples:

- A protest in Taipei concerning Papua New Guinea is a Taiwan event.
- A conference in Cairns attended by PNG officials is an Australia event.
- An Indonesian government statement concerning a PNG shooting is not a second PNG incident.
- A diplomatic objection issued in Beijing is not a street protest in the country being criticised.
- A meeting in Singapore about Myanmar is not an incident in Myanmar.

Store separately:

- physical_country
- related_country

Only physical_country determines the country incident total.

A related-country article may appear as context only where it creates a direct operational implication.

==================================================
6. VERIFY EVENT DATE, NOT JUST PUBLICATION DATE
==================================================

Use the date on which the event occurred.

Do not automatically use publication date as event date.

Where an article reports:

- An earlier incident
- A continuing investigation
- A funeral
- A response to an earlier death
- A policy discussion arising from an earlier incident
- A retrospective account

the event must retain its original event date.

An older event published during the reporting window must not be counted as a new incident.

If the event date cannot be established:

- Mark event_date as unknown.
- Set date_confidence accordingly.
- Do not describe it as the latest incident.
- Do not include it in daily charts.
- Hold it for review if necessary.

==================================================
7. CREATE A CLASSIFICATION CONFIDENCE GATE
==================================================

Every possible event must receive a classification confidence score from 0 to 100.

Rules:

- 85–100: Include automatically.
- 70–84: Include only if the article clearly describes an event and location.
- 50–69: Hold for manual review.
- Below 50: Exclude.
- Commentary, Background and Not an incident: Always exclude from incident totals.

The generator must not use review-held records in:

- Totals
- Charts
- Top developments
- Main analysis
- Severity calculations
- Maps
- Outlook
- Related incidents

Show held records in an internal review queue.

9. DISTINGUISH INCIDENTS FROM RESPONSES
==================================================

A successful response is not automatically a new security incident.

Examples:

- A tugboat rescues passengers.
- Police recover bodies.
- A captain is thanked for transport assistance.
- Authorities issue clarification.
- A ministry announces an investigation.
- A community leader calls for calm.

Where these refer to an existing incident:

- Link them to the original event.
- Add them as updates.
- Do not count them separately.

A response may become a separate event only where it creates a new operational effect, such as:

- A major road closure
- A curfew
- A port closure
- A flight suspension
- A new police operation affecting movement
- A public protest
- A retaliatory attack

12. DO NOT INVENT OPERATIONAL IMPACT
==================================================

Separate:

- Confirmed operational effect
- Reasonable assessed relevance
- Generic possible consequence

Only confirmed effects may be written as facts.

Example:

Confirmed:

“The incident closed the road for three hours.”

Assessment:

“Further police activity could cause local delays.”

Not acceptable:

“This threatens logistics, utilities and connectivity.”

unless the source or event details support those effects.

Do not automatically claim that every incident affects:

- Staff
- Premises
- Cash movements
- Supply chains
- Airports
- Ports
- Logistics
- Connectivity
- Production
- Customers
- Freedom of movement

Only include an impact where there is a credible causal connection.

13. STOP AUTOMATICALLY GENERATING BUSINESS RISK
==================================================

A security incident does not automatically create commercial impact.

Use three impact levels:

DIRECT IMPACT

A confirmed effect on:

- Staff
- A business site
- A supplier
- A customer
- A transport service
- A delivery route
- Utilities
- Production
- Distribution
- Commercial access

INDIRECT IMPACT

No confirmed business effect, but the event is close enough or significant enough to affect planning.

MONITOR ONLY

Relevant security reporting with no confirmed or likely commercial effect.

The system must assign impact based on evidence.

Do not default every record to Monitor Only simply to fill the map.

If all records are Monitor Only, consider removing the impact legend or replacing the map with a simple incident-location map.

15. PERMANENT BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT RULES
==================================================

The Bottom Line Up Front must not begin with a copied news headline.

Maximum length:

- Two short paragraphs

Paragraph 1 must state:

- Main issue category
- Main affected locations
- Most serious verified development

Paragraph 2 must state:

- Main business concern
- Whether the effect is localised or broader
- Immediate planning priority

Use plain English.

Do not use:

- Operating picture
- Lead development was
- Most operationally significant reporting
- Business exposure centres on
- Easing from an elevated baseline
- While this picture holds
- Principal business risk is direct exposure
- This period looks to be

Approved structure:

“Violent crime and communal violence were the main security concerns during the reporting period, with the most serious incidents recorded in [locations]. [Named incident or short description] was the highest-rated event.

The immediate business concerns are [supported effects]. Current reporting indicates that the risk is [localised or widespread]. Businesses should focus on [specific planning priority].”

Only populate claims supported by the final dataset.

16. DO NOT CLAIM A TREND WITHOUT COMPARISON DATA
==================================================

The system must not state that conditions are:

- Easing
- Worsening
- Improving
- Deteriorating
- Building
- Spreading
- Stabilising
- Accelerating
- Returning to normal
- Remaining elevated
- Falling from a high baseline

unless comparative data exists.

Minimum trend requirement:

- Current reporting period
- Immediately previous equivalent reporting period

Preferred trend requirement:

- Current period
- Previous period
- Four-week average

A trend must be supported by a measurable change in:

- Incident count
- Severity
- Casualties
- Geographic spread
- Operational impact
- Duration
- Frequency

Where no comparative data exists, use neutral wording:

“During the reporting period…”

The pre-publication gate must reject any unsupported trend wording.

20. RECOMMENDATIONS MUST BE PROPORTIONATE
==================================================

Recommendations must be selected from the actual event effects.

Approved standard options:

MOVEMENT

- Confirm current route conditions before travel into affected districts.
- Avoid non-essential after-hours movement where recent violence has occurred.
- Allow extra travel time where police activity or local gatherings are reported.
- Maintain an alternative route where disruption has been confirmed.

SITE SECURITY

- Review access arrangements at sites close to reported incidents.
- Confirm after-hours escalation procedures with guards and site managers.
- Check that emergency contacts and reporting lines remain current.

STAFF AWARENESS

- Brief affected staff on specific locations and current restrictions.
- Reinforce immediate reporting of roadblocks, violence or police activity.
- Share only verified areas to avoid.

TRANSPORT AND LOGISTICS

- Confirm airport, port and road status before affected movements.
- Check supplier and delivery routes where disruption is reported.
- Hold alternatives for time-sensitive journeys.

ESCALATION

- Escalate when violence approaches a business location or staff route.
- Escalate when official movement restrictions affect access.
- Escalate when a direct operational effect is confirmed.

Do not automatically recommend:

- Varying all routines
- Secure transport for every movement
- Treating districts as no-go
- Guard reinforcement
- Hardened perimeters
- Movement restrictions
- Site closure
- Crisis-plan activation
- Work from home
- Nationwide travel restrictions

These stronger measures require event-specific evidence.
