REDESIGN SHIPPING WATCH — MONTHLY PRODUCT

Shipping Watch is a MONTHLY report. The current version is not fit for purpose because it is structured like a weekly incident report and over-focuses on individual events.

Redesign the product around MONTH-ON-MONTH CHANGE, TREND, OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE and NEXT-30-DAY OUTLOOK.

DO NOT simply restyle the existing PDF. Change the report logic, data structure, prompts and export layout.

CORE PURPOSE

Shipping Watch should answer:

1. What materially changed during the month?
2. Where did maritime/security risk increase or decrease?
3. What trends are developing?
4. What are the operational and commercial consequences?
5. What should shipping, security, logistics and supply-chain teams watch during the next 30 days?

REPORTING PERIOD

Use a full calendar month or clearly defined monthly reporting period.

Do not use a 7-day reporting structure.

Where possible compare:
- Current month
- Previous month
- 3-month average

Do not invent comparisons where historical data is unavailable.

PAGE 5 — COMMERCIAL & OPERATIONAL IMPACT
==================================================

This page must assess whether maritime-security developments are producing real commercial or operational effects.

Track where evidence exists:

- Route deviation
- Transit volumes
- Transit delays
- Port disruption
- War-risk premiums
- Marine insurance
- Freight rates
- Chartering
- Bunker / fuel effects
- Cargo security
- Landside logistics
- Additional security costs

IMPORTANT RULE:

NO DATA = OMIT THE METRIC.

Do NOT fill the page with repeated statements such as:

"Not established in available reporting."

Only include categories for which meaningful information exists.

Use evidence-led language.

Example:

COMMERCIAL SIGNAL

Hormuz transit volumes declined during the reporting period. Available reporting does not yet demonstrate a corresponding material increase in APAC freight rates.

Distinguish clearly between:

CONFIRMED IMPACT
EMERGING SIGNAL
NO MATERIAL CHANGE IDENTIFIED

PAGE 6 — POLESTAR VIEW: NEXT 30 DAYS
==================================================

Merge the existing "What Matters" and "Polestar View" concepts into ONE strong analytical page.

Sections:

POLESTAR VIEW

A concise current judgement explaining the strategic picture.

OUTLOOK — NEXT 30 DAYS

3-5 analytical points covering the most important expected developments.

WATCH INDICATORS

Specific observable indicators that analysts should monitor.

Examples:

- Increase in attacks outside the current threat concentration
- Major carrier route suspension
- Material reduction in vessel transit volumes
- Change in war-risk premium
- Increase in Singapore/Malacca boarding incidents
- Port closure or access restriction
- Naval escalation affecting commercial routes

ESCALATION TRIGGERS

Identify developments that would materially change the assessment.

Example:

Further commercial-vessel attacks outside the present Hormuz concentration would indicate geographic expansion of the threat.

Do not use generic recommendations such as:

"Continue to monitor the situation."

Every indicator or trigger must be specific.

==================================================
ANALYTICAL RULES
==================================================

1. Shipping Watch is MONTHLY.

2. Prioritise TREND and CHANGE over volume of reporting.

3. One news article does not equal one incident.

4. Multiple reports of the same event must be deduplicated.

5. Do not allow one active theatre, such as the Gulf, to automatically define the risk level for the entire Shipping Watch product.

6. Regional and thematic assessments must be separate.

7. Overall assessments must be based on evidence across the reporting scope.

8. Do not assign Extreme simply because an Extreme incident exists.

9. Incident severity and regional risk are separate assessments.

10. Do not populate pages with "Pending", "Unknown", "No AIS sample", or "Not established" unless analytically important.

11. Where location data is available, resolve the country and region before publication.

12. Minimise "Unknown" geography.

13. Do not duplicate the same analytical judgement across several pages.

14. Every page must add new information.

15. The report must be useful to:
- Corporate security
- Shipping operations
- Port and terminal operators
- Logistics teams
- Supply-chain teams
- Business continuity teams
- Risk managers
- Commercial leadership
