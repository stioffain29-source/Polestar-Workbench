AMENDMENT TO EXISTING APAC WEEKLY AND MIDDLE EAST WEEKLY REPORTS

Do not rebuild or replace the existing report product, structure, export process or styling.

Amend the existing APAC Weekly and Middle East Weekly reports as follows.

1. EXPAND INTELLIGENCE COVERAGE

The reports must not focus only on security incidents.

Add material developments across:

SECURITY
Armed conflict, terrorism, civil unrest, significant crime, maritime security and personnel security.

POLITICAL
Elections, government changes, political instability, major policy decisions, diplomatic tensions, sanctions, interstate tensions and political developments with business implications.

REGULATORY
New or changed legislation, tariffs, sanctions, customs rules, import/export controls, labour regulation, data regulation, cybersecurity regulation, foreign ownership rules, visa or immigration rules, shipping restrictions, energy regulation and other material compliance changes.

ADVERSE WEATHER & NATURAL HAZARDS
Typhoons, cyclones, flooding, extreme heat, wildfire, drought, earthquakes, volcanic activity, landslides and other hazards where there is credible impact on travel, personnel, infrastructure, utilities, sites, ports, airports or supply chains.

CYBER
Major ransomware, state-linked cyber activity, attacks affecting infrastructure, logistics, ports, airlines, utilities, telecoms or government systems, major data breaches and significant cyber regulatory developments.

BUSINESS & OPERATIONAL DISRUPTION
Ports, airports, airspace, roads, borders, shipping, fuel, energy, utilities, telecommunications, supply chains, industrial action and major infrastructure disruption.

Only include developments with credible business or operational relevance.

2. ADD VISUAL INTELLIGENCE

The existing report needs significantly more visual content.

Where data supports it, add:

- Regional hotspot map showing the most significant developments of the week
- Incident or development map by location
- Weekly developments by category chart
- Four week trend graph
- Country or sub-region activity chart
- Weather impact map where relevant
- Political or regulatory timeline where relevant
- 7 day forward watchlist timeline
- Operational disruption map where relevant
- Maritime, airspace or transport map where relevant

Do not add charts simply as decoration.

Each visual must answer at least one of:

WHERE is activity occurring?
WHAT type of risk is driving the week?
IS activity increasing or decreasing?
WHEN is the next significant event?
WHAT operational exposure does it create?

Charts must use real underlying data. Do not invent values to populate visuals.

3. EVENT DEDUPLICATION

Multiple articles covering the same underlying event must be consolidated into ONE development.

Do not create separate Key Developments because Reuters, local media, trade press and other sources reported the same event.

Cluster reporting by EVENT, then use multiple credible sources to build one consolidated assessment.

4. KEY DEVELOPMENTS

Maintain approximately 5 to 8 genuinely separate developments.

Ensure the section reflects the full regional picture rather than being dominated by repeated reporting on one subject.

Selection should consider:

Security
Political
Regulatory
Weather
Cyber
Operational disruption

Not every category needs to appear every week. Only include material developments.

5. 7 DAY WATCHLIST

Expand the existing watchlist beyond security incidents.

It may include:

Scheduled protests
Elections
Political deadlines
Court rulings
Legislative votes
New regulations entering force
Sanctions deadlines
Industrial action
Port or transport disruption
Major public events
Conflict escalation indicators
Storm landfall windows
Flood or severe weather forecasts
Cyber threat developments

Use a visual timeline where practical.

6. POLESTAR OUTLOOK

The existing Polestar Outlook must assess the overall regional operating environment.

It must not default to comments such as:

“Nothing useful came through on public order this week.”

If one intelligence category is quiet, assess the other material political, regulatory, security, operational, weather or cyber developments.

The Outlook should explain:

What is likely to matter next week
Where conditions may deteriorate
What indicators should be monitored
What could change the current assessment
Potential business and operational consequences

7. CORE EDITORIAL TEST

For every potential item ask:

DOES THIS MATTER TO BUSINESS OPERATIONS?

Relevant impacts include:

People
Travel
Sites
Assets
Supply chains
Transport
Energy
Utilities
Compliance
Regulation
Business continuity
Corporate exposure

If there is no credible operational relevance, exclude it.

Do not change the existing report branding, PDF layout, page structure or export workflow unless required to accommodate these additional intelligence categories and visuals.
