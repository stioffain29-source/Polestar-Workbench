Polestar Workbench Information Collection Plan

Purpose

The purpose of this plan is to provide a simple, repeatable method for collecting and maintaining information used in the Polestar Workbench.

The aim is not to collect everything. The aim is to collect information that helps explain operational risk across fuel, fertiliser, civil unrest, energy, shipping, cargo, strikes, and Papua / PNG security conditions.

Collection Cycle

Collect → Check → Categorise → Rate severity → Add to dashboard → Review → Report

Core Collection Rules

1. Do not add information that cannot be sourced.
2. Do not invent missing coordinates.
3. Do not use social media as sole confirmation for serious incidents unless clearly marked as unverified.
4. Do not duplicate existing records.
5. Use the agreed categories and severity ratings.
6. Keep titles factual and short.
7. Keep summaries operational, not dramatic.
8. If in doubt, hold the record back for review rather than forcing it into the dashboard.

Severity Ratings

Use only:

- Extreme
- High
- Moderate
- Low
- Insignificant

Categories

Use only:

- Fuel

Topic Collection Requirements

Fuel

Requirement:
Track fuel shortages, price shocks, refinery disruption, tanker disruption, subsidy changes, fuel protests and supply interruptions.

Indicators:
- queues at fuel stations
- price spikes
- subsidy removal
- refinery outages
- tanker disruption
- fuel depot protests
- transport strikes linked to fuel prices
- disruption to commercial transport or logistics

Sources:
- local media
- energy trade press
- government energy ministries
- oil and gas industry sources
- port and shipping reports
- company statements
- social media for early warning only
- GDELT for broad media monitoring and trend scanning

Frequency:
Daily scan, event-driven update, weekly summary.

Dashboard action:
Add record if the issue affects supply, pricing, transport, public order or business continuity.

Fertiliser

Requirement:
Track fertiliser supply, pricing, export bans, subsidy changes, ammonia/urea disruption and agricultural impact.

Indicators:
- export restrictions
- price increases
- shortages before planting seasons
- port delays affecting fertiliser shipments
- factory outages
- farmer protests linked to fertiliser
- disruption to food production or food security

Sources:
- fertiliser trade publications
- agriculture ministries
- commodity news
- food security reporting
- local media
- shipping and port sources
- GDELT for broad media monitoring

Frequency:
Weekly scan, daily if market is moving, event-driven for major disruptions.

Dashboard action:
Add record if it affects food security, pricing, planting cycles, supply availability or agricultural stability.

Civil Unrest / Protests

Requirement:
Track protests, strikes, riots, demonstrations, labour action, politically driven unrest and public disorder.

Indicators:
- protests near government sites
- roadblocks
- transport disruption
- labour strikes
- police deployment
- arrests
- violence between protesters and police
- election-related unrest
- unrest linked to food, fuel, wages, religion or ethnic tension

Sources:
- local media
- police updates
- government statements
- social media for early warning
- embassy alerts
- GDELT for broad media/event monitoring
- company security reports
- trusted local contacts

Frequency:
Daily scan, event-driven update.

Dashboard action:
Add record if there is disruption, violence, arrests, road closures, business impact or escalation potential.

Indicators:
- blackouts
- load shedding
- generation shortfall
- grid alerts
- substation fires
- power station disruption
- fuel shortages affecting power generation
- public protest over power supply

Sources:

Dashboard action:
Add record if it affects operations, production, logistics, communications, public order or business continuity.

Indicators:

- port closure
- vessel attacks
- route diversion

Dashboard action:
Add record if it affects routes, ports, vessels, freight cost, delays or client supply chains.

Dashboard action:

Land Strikes

Requirement:
Track drone, missile, rocket and related strike activity on land-based targets in the Middle East and Gulf region.

Indicators:
- drone attacks
- missile launches
- air defence interceptions
- damage to airports, energy infrastructure, ports, military sites or civilian areas
- escalation statements
- repeated attacks against the same sector or location
- attacks affecting business continuity, travel, aviation or energy supply

Sources:
- government statements
- military/security advisories
- credible news wires
- regional media
- aviation/maritime advisories where relevant
- social media only as early warning
- GDELT for broad media monitoring

Frequency:
Daily scan, event-driven update.

Dashboard action:
Add to strike tracker if the incident has a date, country, target type and credible source.

Indicators:

Daily scan, event-driven update.

Dashboard action:
Add to maritime tracker if it affects a vessel, port, maritime route or chokepoint.

Flashpoint / Other

Requirement:
Capture emerging issues that do not yet fit cleanly into another topic but may become operationally relevant.

Indicators:
- sudden political tension
- emerging conflict indicators
- sanctions or diplomatic escalation
- severe weather with operational impact
- border disruption
- sudden regulatory action
- public disorder not yet categorised
- weak signals from multiple sources

Sources:
- local media
- regional media
- government statements
- embassy alerts
- GDELT for wide-area scanning
- social media for early warning only
- trusted local contacts
- company/security reports

Frequency:
Daily scan, weekly review.

Dashboard action:
Add only if operationally relevant. Do not use Other as a dumping ground. If the issue clearly fits another category, move it there.

Validation Standard

Before adding a record, check:

1. Is there a source?
2. Is the date clear?
3. Is the country clear?
4. Is the category correct?
5. Is the severity reasonable?
6. Is the location known?
7. Are coordinates available?
8. Is this already in the dashboard?
9. Is the information operationally useful?

If coordinates are missing, leave them blank. The record can still appear in tables and charts but should not appear on the map.

Source Confidence

High confidence:
- official government or agency statement
- recognised news agency
- reputable trade publication
- direct company or port authority statement
- multiple credible sources reporting the same event

Medium confidence:
- local media with named detail
- specialist industry source
- trusted local contact
- GDELT result supported by another source

Low confidence:
- single social media post
- anonymous Telegram claim
- vague local report
- unclear location or date
- unsourced repost

Do not treat low-confidence information as confirmed. Hold it for review or mark it clearly.

Escalation Rule

Escalate for review if:

- severity is High or Extreme
- source is social media only
- country is unknown
- location is unclear
- the record is in Other / Flashpoint
- the incident may affect client operations
- there is a possible duplicate
- the event is politically, legally or commercially sensitive
- the event could affect travel, logistics, production, ports, energy or personnel safety

Outputs

The collected information supports:

- dashboard totals
- topic pages

- weekly briefs
- spot reports
- client alerts

Review Rhythm

Daily:
Scan priority sources and add relevant new records.

Weekly:
Review topic trends and remove noise from reporting outputs.

Monthly:
Review source quality, categories and recurring data gaps.

GDELT Use

Use GDELT as a broad scanning tool, not as the final source by itself.

Use it to:
- identify emerging media patterns
- spot countries or topics gaining attention
- find early indicators of unrest, disruption or escalation
- support flashpoint monitoring
- help locate original media reporting

Do not use GDELT alone as confirmation for serious incidents. Where possible, trace the event back to the original article, government notice, trade source or trusted local source.

Golden Rule

The dashboard should be useful before it is comprehensive.

Bad data is worse than missing data.
