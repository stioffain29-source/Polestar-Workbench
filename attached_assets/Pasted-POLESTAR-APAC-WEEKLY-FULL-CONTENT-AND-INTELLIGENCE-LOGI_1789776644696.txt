3. EDITORIAL RELEVANCE FILTER
==================================================

Before an item is allowed into the report ask:

WOULD A REGIONAL SECURITY, RISK, RESILIENCE, OPERATIONS, LOGISTICS, TRAVEL OR COMMERCIAL LEADER REASONABLY NEED TO KNOW THIS?

If NO:
DROP IT.

Normally exclude:

routine traffic enforcement
minor local crime
minor accidents
routine police activity
local community stories
small administrative actions
minor enforcement stories
isolated events with no wider operational consequence

CURRENT FAILURE EXAMPLE:

Philippines
Local crackdown on illegal drag racing in Mandaue City

This should NOT have appeared as a major APAC development.

It has very limited regional business relevance.

CURRENT FAILURE EXAMPLE:

Thailand
Bomb and small arms attack on a territorial defence volunteer vehicle in Narathiwat

This is a genuine security development and should have been identified and assessed.

The selection system must not allow a minor traffic enforcement story to outrank or displace a bombing and armed attack.

==================================================
4. DEDUPLICATION
==================================================

ONE EVENT = ONE DEVELOPMENT.

Multiple articles about the same event are supporting sources.

They are NOT separate developments.

Cluster using:

country
location
date
actors
underlying incident
policy decision
operational consequence

Examples:

Five Philippines stories about one protest = ONE development.

Four Indian articles about one fuel duty change = ONE development.

Six articles about one bombing = ONE development.

Do not allow source volume to increase editorial importance.

==================================================
5. SEVERITY LOGIC
==================================================

Use CURRENT SEVERITY only.

Use only:

INSIGNIFICANT
LOW
MODERATE
HIGH
EXTREME

Severity is NOT the same as importance.

Severity must reflect the CURRENT OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE.

INSIGNIFICANT

Minimal impact.
No meaningful effect on business operations, people or access.

LOW

Limited local effect.
Mostly inconvenience.
Routine or isolated.
Little or no wider operational consequence.

MODERATE

Credible local operational impact.
May require travel, staffing, access, logistics or compliance adjustments.

HIGH

Significant violence, disruption, restriction or operational effect.
Material consequence for people, operations, travel, assets, transport, supply chains or continuity.

EXTREME

Major or widespread disruption.
Major conflict escalation.
Mass casualty event.
Severe infrastructure failure.
Sustained or widespread operational consequences.

==================================================
6. SEVERITY MUST MAKE SENSE
==================================================

Do not assign HIGH because:
police are involved
the headline is dramatic
the article uses words such as crackdown
the event occurred on a road
several outlets reported it

The Mandaue drag racing story should never have been HIGH.

It is at most a limited local issue and probably should have been excluded entirely.

Australian immigration changes should not automatically be HIGH.

A policy creating additional paperwork, eligibility restrictions or inconvenience is normally LOW or MODERATE unless there is evidence of major immediate operational disruption.

No casualties are required for HIGH severity, but there must be a credible significant operational consequence.

A bombing or armed attack must be assessed using:

target
location
casualties
intent
wider threat
access impact
persistence
security response
potential for further attacks

Do not treat a local enforcement story and an armed attack as broadly equivalent simply because both have been assigned the same severity.

==================================================
7. MATERIALITY RANKING
==================================================

After relevance filtering and severity assessment, rank developments using:

1. actual operational consequence
2. impact on people and travel
3. impact on sites and assets
4. conflict, terrorism or serious violence
5. major political or regulatory consequence
6. transport, logistics or supply chain effect
7. energy or utilities effect
8. significant natural hazard
9. cyber impact
10. forward relevance over the next seven days
11. regional or cross border significance

Severity is only ONE factor.

A HIGH local incident can rank below a MODERATE regulatory, transport, energy or weather development if the latter has materially greater business consequences.
