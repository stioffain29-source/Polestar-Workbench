AMENDMENT TO EXISTING APAC WEEKLY / MIDDLE EAST WEEKLY GENERATION LOGIC

DO NOT rebuild the product.

DO NOT change the existing PDF structure, branding or export workflow.

The current report is incorrect because it is still generating an INCIDENT LIST from the scraped feed and merely assigning category labels to stories.

This must change.

THE WEEKLY REPORT IS A CURATED REGIONAL INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT, NOT AN INCIDENT DATABASE EXPORT.

1. STOP USING THE INCIDENT FEED AS THE REPORT STRUCTURE

Do not select the highest number of scraped incidents and print them sequentially.

Do not attempt to fill the report by taking individual articles and classifying them as:

Security
Operational Disruption
Political
Regulatory
Weather
Cyber

Instead:

FIRST identify the material regional DEVELOPMENTS during the reporting period.

THEN group source articles into those developments.

THEN select only developments that materially affect the regional business operating environment.

A development may be supported by multiple source articles but must appear only once.

2. THE REPORT MUST ACTIVELY SEARCH EACH INTELLIGENCE DOMAIN

Do not rely on the existing security / protest incident feed to discover political, regulatory, weather or cyber developments.

The generation process must actively search / query sources separately for:

SECURITY & CONFLICT

POLITICAL

REGULATORY

ADVERSE WEATHER & NATURAL HAZARDS

CYBER & INFORMATION RISK

BUSINESS & OPERATIONAL DISRUPTION

Each domain requires its own discovery pass before the report is written.

The absence of items in the general incident feed does NOT mean there were no political, regulatory, weather or cyber developments.

3. POLITICAL COVERAGE

Actively identify significant political developments during the reporting period.

Relevant examples include:

Elections and election results
Government or cabinet changes
Coalition instability
Leadership changes
Major parliamentary developments
Important government policy announcements
Political crises
Diplomatic tensions
Interstate relations
Sanctions
Major bilateral agreements
Defence or security agreements
Political decisions affecting foreign companies
Government intervention affecting markets or operations

Do not classify a routine protest story as the sole political item.

The political section should explain:

WHAT changed politically
WHY it matters to business
WHAT could happen next

4. REGULATORY COVERAGE

Actively search for actual regulatory developments.

Relevant items include:

New legislation
Regulatory changes
Sanctions
Tariffs
Customs changes
Import restrictions
Export controls
Foreign ownership rules
Labour legislation
Visa / immigration changes
Data regulation
Cybersecurity regulation
AI regulation
Environmental rules
Energy regulation
Shipping regulation
Port rules
Aviation regulation
Tax changes
Compliance requirements
Licensing changes
Market access restrictions

A criminal investigation, corruption investigation or visa fraud case is NOT automatically a regulatory development.

Only classify something as Regulatory when there has been an actual or proposed change to rules, legislation, enforcement requirements or business obligations.

For each regulatory development explain:

WHAT changed
WHEN it takes effect
WHO is affected
WHAT businesses may need to do

5. ADVERSE WEATHER & NATURAL HAZARDS

Actively search current regional weather and hazard sources.

Include:

Typhoons
Cyclones
Tropical storms
Flooding
Monsoon disruption
Extreme rainfall
Heatwaves
Wildfires
Haze
Drought
Earthquakes
Volcanic activity
Landslides
Tsunami warnings
Major adverse weather systems

A charity fundraising story about historic flooding is NOT a weather development.

Weather items must relate to a CURRENT or FORWARD operational hazard.

Prioritise:

Airport disruption
Port disruption
Road closures
Site access
Power disruption
Personnel exposure
Supply chain disruption
Manufacturing exposure

Include forecast information where it materially affects the next seven days.

6. CYBER COVERAGE

Cyber must be actively searched every week.

Do not omit Cyber simply because no cyber story entered the normal incident feed.

Look for:

Major ransomware
State sponsored cyber activity
Critical infrastructure attacks
Telecommunications disruption
Major data breaches
Government network compromise
Attacks affecting ports
Attacks affecting airlines
Attacks affecting logistics
Attacks affecting financial services
Attacks affecting utilities
Major cloud / digital infrastructure outages
New cyber regulation
Material regional threat campaigns

Only include material developments.

If no material cyber development is found after a dedicated search, state:

NO MATERIAL REGIONAL CYBER DEVELOPMENT IDENTIFIED DURING THIS REPORTING PERIOD.

Do not fabricate an item merely to populate the category.

7. REMOVE LOW VALUE INCIDENT NOISE

The report must aggressively reject stories that do not contribute to understanding the regional operating environment.

Exclude examples such as:

Anniversary articles
Historical retrospectives
Opinion pieces with no current development
Local murders with no wider operational relevance
Charity fundraising stories
Religious events with no operational impact
Generic terrorism commentary
Minor isolated crime
Routine police activity
Duplicate protest reporting
Multiple articles covering the same event
Social media reposts of an event already captured from a credible source

The fact that a story contains words such as:

attack
terrorism
protest
security
flood
fuel
visa

does NOT automatically make it relevant.

8. DEDUPLICATE AGGRESSIVELY

Several articles about the same event must become ONE intelligence development.

Examples:

Five articles about the same Bangkok protest = ONE development.

Several articles covering the same MANIBELA strike = ONE development.

Multiple sources covering the same Myanmar airport attack = ONE development.

Different sources are supporting evidence, NOT separate report entries.

9. BALANCE THE REGIONAL PICTURE

Do not allow one country to dominate simply because its media environment produces more searchable articles.

The current report is heavily skewed toward the Philippines because the system is counting source volume rather than regional importance.

Selection must be based on MATERIALITY, not article count.

The final report should represent the significant regional picture across APAC / Middle East.

There is NO requirement to include every country.

There IS a requirement to avoid mistaking source volume for risk importance.

10. KEY DEVELOPMENTS

Reduce Key Developments to approximately 6–10 genuinely important developments.

They should represent the most consequential developments across the entire regional risk picture.

A typical report might contain:

2 Security / Conflict
1–2 Political
1–2 Regulatory / Policy
1 Weather / Natural Hazard
1 Operational / Infrastructure
1 Cyber

THIS IS NOT A FIXED QUOTA.

Do not manufacture weak items to fill categories.

But the system must have actively searched every category before deciding there is nothing material to include.

11. CREATE DISTINCT INTELLIGENCE SECTIONS

The report should contain concise analytical sections for:

SECURITY & CONFLICT

POLITICAL & GEOPOLITICAL

REGULATORY & POLICY

WEATHER & NATURAL HAZARDS

CYBER & INFORMATION RISK

BUSINESS & OPERATIONAL DISRUPTION

These sections should summarise the regional picture.

They must NOT simply repeat the Key Developments verbatim.

If a domain genuinely has no material development, use one short sentence saying so rather than filling the section with irrelevant content.

12. BLUF MUST USE ALL INTELLIGENCE DOMAINS

The BLUF must not default to public order or protest reporting.

It should identify the 3–5 developments that most changed the APAC / Middle East operating environment during the reporting period regardless of category.

The BLUF should potentially draw from:

Political
Regulatory
Security
Conflict
Weather
Cyber
Infrastructure
Energy
Transport
Supply chain

13. CHARTS MUST REPRESENT THE CURATED REPORT

Do not create charts from every scraped article.

The existing chart showing counts such as:

Security 33
Operational Disruption 21
Regulatory 1
Political 1

is measuring the SCRAPE, not the regional risk environment.

Charts should use the CURATED intelligence dataset after deduplication and relevance filtering.

Suitable visuals include:

Material developments by domain
Material developments by sub-region
Four week trend by domain
Current regional risk themes
Weather systems affecting operations
Political / regulatory timeline
7 day forward watchlist

Do not visualise junk data.

14. FINAL QUALITY TEST

Before generating the PDF, ask:

Does this read like a regional intelligence briefing for a corporate security / risk / operations leader?

OR

Does it read like a long list of news incidents?

If it reads like an incident list, DO NOT EXPORT.

Re-run curation and synthesis first.

The desired product is:

A concise assessment of the regional operating environment covering:

Security
Politics
Geopolitics
Regulation
Weather
Cyber
Business disruption

It is NOT:

A twenty page list of scraped security incidents.
