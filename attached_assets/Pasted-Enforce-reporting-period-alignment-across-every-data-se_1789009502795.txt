Enforce reporting period alignment across every data series. A metric outside the report window must never be described as a weekly movement for that window.
Separate current period evidence from lagged benchmark data. Older series can provide context, but the prose must clearly label them as lagged rather than treating them as contemporaneous.
Strengthen the event relevance gate. Fuel Watch should only admit events with a demonstrable link to fuel price, production, refining, storage, distribution, availability, transport economics or supply continuity.
Do not admit stories merely because they mention oil, tankers, Hormuz, refineries, energy or fuel related entities. The system must establish the operational fuel consequence first.
Validate physical geography independently from article subject, publisher location or named entities. Geography must represent where the relevant fuel event actually occurred.
Require evidence before causal language. The prose must not claim an event caused rerouting, shortages, higher costs or availability problems unless the underlying evidence supports that relationship.
Stop generating generic market boilerplate. Every statement about shortages, crack spreads, rationing, route pressure, exports or availability must be traceable to validated evidence in that reporting window.
Build analytical prose from specific validated developments, not generic templates. Each paragraph should explain what changed, why it matters, where the exposure sits and likely direction.
Prevent raw feed language reaching the client output. Titles, malformed capitalisation, publisher fragments, vague headlines and incomplete sentences must be rewritten or rejected before render.
Add a cross section consistency check. Executive Summary, What Matters, Regional Highlights and Polestar View must not identify different priority geographies or developments without explaining the change in judgement.
Stop phrases such as “the confirmed change” unless the actual change is named. Client prose should always state the event directly.
Watch Next must only contain signals that are grounded in the report evidence or a validated forward indicator. Do not introduce new countries or themes that have not been established elsewhere in the analysis.
Separate backend confidence from analytical confidence. Model uncertainty, unresolved fields and validation status must remain internal unless Polestar deliberately chooses to express an analytical confidence level.
Add a final evidence to prose audit before render: every material analytical claim should trace back to a validated record, metric or forward signal.
Apply these controls as shared report architecture, not Fuel Watch specific patches, so the same failures cannot recur under different headlines, countries or commodities.
