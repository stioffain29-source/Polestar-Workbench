---
name: Publication gates that block whole reports
description: Where Workbench publication gates used to fail on factually correct reporting, and the rules now in force that keep them fail-closed only for unsupported or fabricated content.
---

Publication validation is fail-closed across Workbench reports: a failed check stops the build
or the export rather than shipping unverified text. Three rules previously failed on content
that was correct. Owner ruling: a gate exists to catch unsupported or fabricated content, never
to block a true report.

## Forward-watch observances are publishable

A region's real 7-day calendar can be legitimately holiday-led (late-September Middle East:
Saudi National Day, Yemen Revolution Day, Sukkot). Holidays are operational information —
closures, reduced cover, payment cut-offs — so a holiday-led week now publishes the calendar it
has and states that no other significant scheduled events were identified. The item-count range
still applies.

**Why:** the watch list is COLLECTED, not written, so its rule throws before any analysis spend
and no re-ask can invent forward events the calendar does not contain. Failing the build must be
reserved for output that would be wrong, not merely thin.

**How to apply:** the note is one shared helper rendered in BOTH the preview and the PDF after
the watch rows; its mixed-list wording must never deny the non-holiday events also rendered.

## Percentage tracing binds to the claim, not to a bag of numbers

Only a percentage presented as a MOVEMENT IN A BENCHMARK PRICE SERIES is traced against the
calculated price feed, because that is the only kind of figure the feed can confirm. A
percentage about a volume, flow, run rate, tariff, margin or share is evidence, not a market
claim, and is left to the evidence checks.

**Why:** the gate exists to stop invented market numbers, and a source-quoted tariff or run rate
is untraceable to the price feed yet perfectly true.

**How to apply:** read the SUBJECT from the words BEFORE the figure — a non-price noun later in
the clause must not excuse a fabricated move ("Brent fell 8% because supply increased"). Never
accept a figure just because some unrelated source record happens to contain the same number;
numeric coincidence is not provenance.

## Cross-section repetition is consolidated, not blocked

Auto-built sections that both lead with the same event (Situation synthesis and the dated What
Happened record) collide by construction, not by model error. The builder now folds the
duplication before the audit sees it, using the SAME exported predicate the audit applies, so
the fix and the check cannot drift apart.

**Why:** blocking an export because the report states a true fact twice punishes correct
reporting; the fact simply belongs in one place.

**How to apply:** drop the synthesis sentence, not the dated record — a record carries its date,
detail and evidence id, and losing it silently removes evidence from the register. Only drop a
record when the synthesis would otherwise be left with nothing, and never leave either section
empty.
