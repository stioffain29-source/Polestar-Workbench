CURRENT SITUATION
Security incidents in Papua New Guinea during the reporting period were concentrated in Morobe and
Papua New Guinea. Violent crime and maritime were the principal concerns. Morobe generated repeat
reporting within the period, so the pressure there is sustained rather than a one-off. The incidents do not
currently form a single nationwide pattern; risk varies by location, and movement decisions should be
based on current conditions at the destination.

OPERATIONAL IMPACT
No fresh incident-driven business impact was identified this period. Standing exposures - urban crime,
Highlands tribal violence, and intermittent road, power and connectivity disruption - continue to apply.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
MOVEMENT

- Confirm current route conditions before travel into affected districts.
- Avoid non-essential after-hours movement where recent violence has occurred.
SITE SECURITY

- Review access arrangements at sites close to reported incidents.
- Confirm after-hours escalation procedures with guards and site managers.
- Check that emergency contacts and reporting lines remain current.
STAFF AWARENESS

- Reinforce immediate reporting of roadblocks, violence or police activity.

OUTLOOK: NEXT SEVEN DAYS
Violent crime is likely to remain the main concern during the next seven days. Morobe saw repeat reporting

the previous period. The coming days should be judged against that direction rather than any single event.
ESCALATION INDICATORS

- Further casualty-bearing or higher-severity violence close to staffed premises
- Spread of incidents beyond Lae / Morobe and Port Moresby / National Capital District into new districts
- Flashpoints around paydays, court rulings, election cycles and tribal-payback events

POLESTAR VIEW
Weighed together, the week's reporting bears most on both movement planning and the safety of staff and
premises, and that is where checks will pay off first. On assessment, the risk is localised and varies by
location, and it is best managed through location-specific decisions rather than country-wide measures.
The key judgement is that violent crime drives the current concern, while other categories contributed
limited, separate disruption. Businesses should prioritise verified route and site information for Morobe and
Papua New Guinea over broad precautionary measures.
