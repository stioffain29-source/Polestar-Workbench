     REGIONAL OUTLOOK

     Eastern Japan is this week's clearest business exposure because Typhoon Dujuan is already bringing heavy
     rain and transport disruption into a large operating market. That creates immediate friction for staff
     movement, deliveries and time-sensitive services even before any wider knock-on effects are known.

     A different operating strain is emerging in Nepal, where a severe LPG shortage linked to import failures
     points to a slower but broader constraint on fuel-dependent activity. By contrast, the Kohat
     police-headquarters attack in Pakistan is the week's sharpest security signal, but any spillover into
     commercial access or wider operations remains unconfirmed from the reported facts.

     REGIONAL RISK PICTURE

     The most direct transmission into business activity comes from weather-linked transport disruption. In
     eastern Japan, heavy rain and disrupted transport can immediately delay commuting, field activity and
     scheduled cargo movements. Sri Lanka's landslide and flooding on Thailand's Highway 401 show the same
     mechanism on a narrower geographic scale: road access is curtailed, so journey times, routing and site
     reachability become operational issues.

     Nepal's LPG shortage works differently. It is not a sudden closure event, but a fuel-availability problem tied
     to import failures, which can constrain routine operations and distribution if the planned daily gas-bullet
     imports do not ease supply.

     Cyber and security exposures are more conditional this week. The LTFRB platform outage confirms digital
     service disruption and a possible data-exposure issue, but it does not establish wider transport stoppages.
     The Kohat attack demonstrates acute security risk at a police site; any business-access disruption beyond
     that would need separate confirmation.

        PAKISTAN | KOHAT
        KOHAT POLICE HEADQUARTERS ATTACKED
        Security · Extreme · 18 Sep 2026
        ASSESSMENT: An explosion and gun attack hit a police headquarters in Kohat, and the death toll rose to at least 31 including 16 police personnel.

        POLESTAR VIEW: The attack shows acute local security stress. Broader commercial disruption remains a contingent risk, not an established

        7-DAY INDICATOR: Watch for official confirmation of any cordons, access restrictions or service suspensions beyond the police-headquarters area.



        JAPAN | EASTERN JAPAN
        TYPHOON DUJUAN DISRUPTS EASTERN TRANSPORT
        Weather & Natural Hazards · Moderate · 21 Sep 2026
        ASSESSMENT: Typhoon Dujuan was approaching eastern Japan with heavy rain and transport disruption.
        POLESTAR VIEW: This is the week's most direct regional business disruption because transport impacts are already reported. The scale of prolonged
        interruption is still unresolved.
        7-DAY INDICATOR: Watch for reports of broader transport suspensions or evidence that disruption remains limited despite the approaching typhoon.



        NEPAL

        Energy · Moderate · 20 Sep 2026

        POLESTAR VIEW: The shortage is a direct operating constraint, but the planned daily gas-bullet imports could ease pressure if implementation is
        visible.
        7-DAY INDICATOR: Watch for evidence that the planned 160 daily gas-bullet imports are being executed and improving availability.

        Weather & Natural Hazards · Moderate · 19 Sep 2026

     BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS

     Operations teams should prepare temporary schedule compression and remote-work substitutions where
     travel depends on disrupted transport or blocked roads. That decision is grounded in Typhoon Dujuan's
     impact in eastern Japan, the Hatton Bogawantalawa route cutoff in Sri Lanka and flooding that left small
     vehicles unable to use Highway 401 in Surat Thani.

     Logistics managers should review fuel-dependent delivery plans and prioritize essential loads if Nepal's
     LPG shortage persists. The trigger is the confirmed severe shortage linked to import failures, while the
     planned 160 daily gas-bullet imports are the main near-term sign of relief rather than proof of
     normalization.

     IT and compliance teams should route any LTFRB-dependent transactions through manual contingencies
     and assess exposure to delayed regulatory processing while the platform remains offline. The immediate
     trigger is the confirmed outage during a data-breach probe; wider effects on transport activity or data
     compromise are still uncertain.

     POLESTAR OUTLOOK

     The most important unresolved risk is whether Japan's current transport disruption broadens beyond
