     REGIONAL OUTLOOK

     The week's most consequential exposure was the direct interruption at Riyadh airport, where delays and
     cancellations were recorded before operations returned to normal. That stands out because it immediately
     affected staff movement and time-sensitive journeys through a major regional hub, even though the
     disruption was not sustained.

     A separate operating issue is the concentration of attacks touching Gulf energy infrastructure. Confirmed
     damage to Saudi East-West pipeline stations is the strongest signal because physical harm to a strategic
     asset is established, while the Bahrain pipeline attack and Kuwait refinery attack extend the regional
     watchlist without yet confirming service loss. Together, these incidents keep continuity and
     asset-protection planning elevated beyond a single transport disruption.

     REGIONAL RISK PICTURE

     The clearest transmission into business activity came from Riyadh airport. Recorded delays and
     cancellations affected flight availability and could complicate staff travel or time-sensitive shipments if
     disruption recurs. With normal operations reported, a continuing outage or residual backlog should not be
     assumed.

     The Saudi pipeline-station attack presents a different risk path. Physical damage to energy infrastructure is
     confirmed, but the facts do not establish service interruption. Even so, the combination of that damage with
     attacks involving a Bahrain pipeline and a Kuwait refinery raises contingent exposure for operators,
     contractors and industrial users if further incidents begin to affect facility availability.

     Additional Saudi security incidents sharpen the operating backdrop, but the reported facts do not by
     themselves show wider commercial disruption at those locations.

     BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS

     Travel teams can retain alternative routings for staff using Riyadh, but normal airport operations were
     subsequently reported. Renewed cancellations or official restriction notices, rather than an assumed
     continuing outage, should trigger changes to journeys and staff rotations through the airport.

     Asset and site-security teams should review protection measures and maintenance access assumptions
     around strategic facilities, triggered by confirmed damage to Saudi pipeline stations and attacks on a
     Bahrain pipeline and a Kuwait refinery. Since wider outages are not confirmed, resilience checks are better
     grounded than output-adjustment decisions.

     Regional continuity leads should tighten thresholds for escalating incident response, triggered by multiple
     Saudi security incidents alongside infrastructure attacks in neighbouring markets. The key decision is when
     to shift from monitoring to cross-functional coordination if further verified attacks begin affecting
     commercial transport or operating assets directly.

     POLESTAR OUTLOOK

     The priority for the coming week is whether attacks begin to produce sustained service losses, rather than
     remaining a series of localised security shocks. Riyadh airport's recovery is a stabilising signal; confirmed
     damage to Saudi pipeline stations leaves a different test, centred on repairs and continuity. Attacks
     involving energy assets in Bahrain and Kuwait could widen supply exposure if damage, lower throughput or
     extended shutdowns are verified.

     Aviation and energy should therefore be judged by separate operating indicators: repeated cancellations
     would worsen mobility risk, while confirmed processing or pipeline interruptions would matter more for
     industrial continuity. The fatal drone attack and damaged military aircraft warrant follow-on monitoring, not
     assumed commercial interruption. Shipping restrictions or regulatory decisions affecting flows also require
     separate confirmation. Clear restoration notices, normal transport schedules and evidence that damaged
     assets remain serviceable would ease the assessment. Further attacks accompanied by sustained
     closures or verified output losses would materially worsen it.
